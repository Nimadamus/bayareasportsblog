The 2026 49ers Roster and Depth Chart, Position by Position

Who starts, who backs them up, who is hurt, and which position group is one injury from becoming a problem. Updated through the season.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · 49ers

A depth chart in August is a work of fiction with a long revision history, so treat this as a running record rather than a verdict. It gets updated as the roster moves.

Quarterback. Brock Purdy and nobody you want to think about. The single largest gap between the starter and the backup on the roster, and the reason every conversation about this season eventually becomes a conversation about protection.

Running back. Christian McCaffrey when healthy, which is the qualifier that has followed him here. Patrick Taylor Jr and the rest of the room have been getting real camp work, partly by necessity. The offense changes character entirely depending on which version of this group is available in December.

Receiver. The most rebuilt group on the team, twice over. Mike Evans and Christian Kirk came in as veterans. Deebo Samuel returned on a one-year deal. De'Zhaun Stribling has been the story of camp as a rookie. Ricky Pearsall is out for the season after PCL surgery, which is the injury that reshaped this room. KhaDarel Hodge and Demarcus Robinson are the depth, and both have already had moments worth writing about in August.

Tight end. George Kittle, still the most reliable thing on the roster, with Jake Deguara among the summer additions behind him.

Offensive line. The group nobody wants to discuss and everybody should. This is where seasons are decided for a team built on outside zone and play action, and it is the unit whose failures are hardest to fix mid-season.

Defensive line. Nick Bosa, plus Ogbo Okoronkwo from the August signing spree. Bosa easing back is the sort of sentence that reads fine in August and terrifies you in November.

Linebacker. Fred Warner and Dre Greenlaw, both back from the injuries that gutted this defence for two years. If those two play sixteen games each, the defence is fine. If they do not, everything else is theoretical.

Secondary. The group most exposed to the depth problem, and the one Raheem Morris will have to scheme around most.

The injury picture. It has been the defining feature of this camp. Twenty players missed one August practice. Our injury column covers the pattern, and it is a pattern rather than bad luck at this point.

Where the depth actually runs out. Three places. Behind the quarterback, where there is no plan anyone wants to execute. On the offensive line, where the drop from starter to backup is the difference between an offense and a problem. And in the secondary, where the scheme asks a lot of players who have not proven they can hold up over seventeen games. Everywhere else this roster can absorb a loss. In those three rooms it cannot.

How to read this page as the season goes. Depth charts published in August describe intentions. What matters is what changes: who moves up when somebody goes down, which rookie stops being a project, and which veteran signing turns out to have been a warning rather than an upgrade. This page tracks those movements rather than restating the official two-deep every week.
