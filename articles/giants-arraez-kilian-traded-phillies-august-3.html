Arraez Is Gone: The Best Hitter We Had Is a Phillie Now

Luis Arraez and Caleb Kilian to Philadelphia for two right-handed arms. That is the sentence. I have read it about nine times this morning and it still lands the same way, which is like somebody turning the lights off in a room I was still sitting in.

He was the only at-bat I trusted

Understand what just left the building. Arraez led the entire National League in hitting. Not a nice little season, not a feel-good story, the actual league lead at .324 with an .800 OPS, four homers and 43 driven in over 105 games, and a fourth All-Star selection while wearing our uniform. One year, twelve million dollars, and he was the single best position player Buster put in that clubhouse.

And I am going to say the thing I have said in this space all summer. When we were down two in the seventh and the lineup was doing its usual impression of nine guys trying to hit a home run against a slider, he was the one guy I wanted up there. He did not chase. He did not sell out. He put the ball in play, he moved the line, he made pitchers work in a lineup that hands out free outs like Halloween candy. That is not a luxury on this roster. That was the entire offensive identity, and now it is on a plane east.

Yes, I asked for this. No, I do not have to enjoy it.

I have been writing sell for weeks. I wrote it Sunday, I wrote it this morning with Ray, and every word of it was right. You do not keep expiring veterans on a team that is eighteen games under. That is how you end up 79-83 next August telling everybody you are close.

But there is something specific about this one. Ray was our best arm, and losing him hurt in a strategy way. Arraez was our best hitter, and losing him hurts in a watching-the-games way. Nobody bought a ticket to see a rebuild. People bought tickets to see him spray line drives into the right-center gap at a ballpark that eats fly balls alive. He was built for Oracle. He was the correct player for this stadium and this city and we had him for one summer.

The return had better be real, part two

Two right-handed pitching prospects come back: Ramon Marquez, who was Philadelphia's fourth-ranked prospect, and Marty Gair. I am going to be honest and say I did not know either name at breakfast, and I am not going to pretend to scout them at you now because I have not seen them throw a pitch.

What I will say is that getting a team's actual No. 4 prospect is not nothing. That is not a lottery ticket and a handshake. That is at least a front office recognizing they had a leverage position and taking the return seriously, which is more than I expected when I woke up. So credit where it is due, and I will keep the receipt on those two names for 2028.

Kilian goes too, and that one is quiet

Caleb Kilian goes with him. Nobody is going to write a thousand words about that and I understand why, but a guy who has been kicking around this organization for years, who has been shuttled up and down more times than anyone should have to be, gets to go somewhere in a pennant race and matter. Good for him. I hope he gets some big outs in October and I hope somebody in Philadelphia figures out what we never quite did.

What is left of the summer

So here is the lineup now. Eldridge plays every day. Basabe plays every day. The kids get the at-bats they should have been getting in June, and I am going to spend August and September watching young players make young player mistakes and telling myself it is the right thing.

It is the right thing. It is also two months of baseball nobody in this city is going to remember, in a season that got interesting for about three weeks in July before the whole thing folded up like a lawn chair.

The part I cannot get past

Somewhere around September, Arraez is going to be hitting third in a real pennant race, and some Phillies broadcaster is going to say he is the best pure hitter in baseball, and they will be right, and he will be in red. We had him. For one season, in the first year of a deal he signed to come here, we had the National League batting leader on our team and we finished eighteen games under .500 around him.

That is not his fault. That is ours. Go Giants, I guess. Somebody go develop those two arms like the season depends on it, because a couple more Augusts like this one and there is not going to be anybody left to trade.
