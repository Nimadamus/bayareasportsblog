The Sharks 2026-27 Schedule and Season Hub: Every Game, Kept Updated

The schedule landed on 16 July and it opens on 1 October against the Florida Panthers at SAP Center. This page tracks the season as it happens.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · Sharks

This is the page that holds the season together. It gets updated as games are played and as coverage publishes, so bookmark this one rather than hunting through the archive.

Opening night. Thursday 1 October 2026, at home at SAP Center, against the Florida Panthers. The full schedule was released on 16 July. Starting a rebuild year against one of the hardest teams in the league to play against is either bad luck or a useful early measurement, and we'll know which within sixty minutes.

The first month is harder than it looks. Fifteen games, and eight of them away, including a six-game eastern trip from 15 to 24 October that runs Nashville, Detroit, Toronto, Montreal, Ottawa and Boston, with the Toronto and Montreal games on consecutive nights. A young team can be buried by that trip before American Thanksgiving, and a good one can announce itself. The Ottawa game on 22 October is the first look at William Eklund in someone else's sweater after the summer trade that brought back the ninth overall pick.

And the last one. The regular season finishes at home against Anaheim on 10 April 2027. If this team is still playing for something that week, the streak the The franchise history page page describes is about to end.

Where the season is being run from. Ryan Warsofsky is the head coach, Mike Grier is the general manager, and the building is SAP Center in downtown San Jose, which, for the record, is the only major professional arena in the Bay Area that hasn't moved cities in the last decade. The Bay Area franchise moves page has the rest of that regional pattern.

What has to be true for this to be a good season. Three things, in order of how much they matter.

One: the goaltending has to be league-average. Yaroslav Askarov posted a .884 save percentage across 47 games last season and the team still won 39. There's no version of a playoff push that starts anywhere other than in the crease, and the depth chart page lays out exactly how big that gap is.

Two: somebody other than Macklin Celebrini has to score. He put up 115 points and the next man had 59. That isn't a supporting cast, that's a solo album. Michael Misa on the second line and Mason Marchment on a five-year deal are the two bets designed to fix it. The Celebrini records and contract page has what he did last year in full.

Three: the young defence has to hold up. Darnell Nurse and Jacob Trouba are here to absorb hard minutes for two or three years. Sam Dickinson and Michael Kesselring are here to become the actual pairing. If the veterans crater and the kids aren't ready, this becomes a long winter regardless of what the top line does.

The realistic target. The Sharks have missed the playoffs seven years running. Last season they gained 34 points on the year before, one of the biggest single-season improvements in franchise history, and still finished eleventh in the West. The gap to a wild card isn't enormous any more. A first playoff appearance since 2019 is genuinely on the table and would be the biggest thing to happen to this franchise in seven years.

The thing that would make it a bad season. Not losing, this team is allowed to lose. It would be Celebrini getting hurt, or the goaltending staying where it is and wasting a year of an elite centre's prime. Development years are cheap. Wasted years of a player like that aren't.

What we'll be publishing. Game coverage through the season, roster updates on the depth chart page as moves happen, and updates to the Celebrini records and contract page as the numbers move. The archive so far is short, The rebuild finally has a pulse is the column that started it, and that changes from October.

Dates worth marking now. Opening night on 1 October against Florida. The first proper measurement of whether the second line works, which will be obvious by American Thanksgiving. The trade deadline, where a team on the bubble has to decide whether it's buying for the first time since 2019. And the end of the season, where either the streak ends at seven or this page gets a very different tone.

The franchise history page is the long view: thirty-five seasons, one Stanley Cup Final, no Cup. The rest of our coverage is on the Sharks hub.
