Giants 9, Reds 4: Tony Vitello Watched a Sweep on Television and the Offense Scored Nine Without Him

Three home runs, a pinch hit double that cleared the bases, six innings from Logan Webb, and a manager sitting in the clubhouse from the first inning on. Third win in a row. I have no idea what to do with this team.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · Giants

The manager was gone before the game had a score. Two outs in the top of the first, Webb pitching, a check swing on Eugenio Suarez that Ben May said was held up, and Tony Vitello got himself thrown out of a getaway day game he had barely watched.

Then the Giants went out and put up nine.

Nine runs. Three home runs. A sweep of the Reds, a third straight win, and the first time since the end of July that this club has strung three together. On a Wednesday afternoon in the last week of August with the season long dead and the manager in the clubhouse. Baseball is a stupid, stupid sport and I love it.

Jonah Cox started it in the second with a ball he hit 421 feet, two runs in, and if you had told me in April that Jonah Cox would be the guy who opened the scoring in a sweep I would have asked you who that was. Cincinnati got one back in the same inning on a Trevino single and Hector Rodriguez tied it in the third with the first of his two solo shots, because of course the one guy in that lineup who could hit today hit two.

Devers untied it in the fifth. Two run shot, 414 feet, his 29th, and his third in three days. He's one away from thirty and I've already said my piece about how little credit he gets for it, so I'll just note the swing and move on.

Shay Whitcomb hit one 410 feet in the sixth for two more. Six to two.

Webb gave them six innings, five hits, two runs, a walk and six strikeouts, and then walked off without a decision because Jason Foley gave one back in the seventh and Spencer Bivens ended up with the win for an inning of work. That's the fourth or fifth time this year Webb has thrown a perfectly good start and gotten nothing on the line next to his name. Nothing about that man's season adds up on paper and he keeps taking the ball every fifth day anyway.

The eighth is the part I'd actually pay to see again. Bases loaded, Tony Santillan on the mound, and Drew Gilbert comes off the bench cold and doubles all three of them in. One for one, three RBI, the whole game in one swing from a guy who was sitting on the bench eating sunflower seeds four minutes earlier. Nine to two.

Rodriguez hit his second one in the ninth to make it look closer than it was. Nine to four. Sweep.

Lodolo took the loss, four innings, four earned, and he never looked comfortable. Twenty something outs later the Reds are 62-72 and heading home having scored one run in the first two games of the series combined.

What I keep chewing on is that this offense has looked like a real major league offense for a week and a half now. Balls in the air, counts worked, guys off the bench contributing, the four hole getting protection for the first time since May. It's late August. It's meaningless. It's also the first stretch all year where I've watched a Giants game and not felt like I was doing a chore.

Fifty five and seventy eight. Twenty three under. Nothing left to play for except the thirty on Devers, whatever Eldridge gives them when he gets back, and finding out which of these kids can actually hit. That's the whole rest of the season and I'll be watching all of it, which probably says more about me than about them.

The manager watched a nine run sweep on a clubhouse television. I don't have a joke for that one. I just think it's funny.
