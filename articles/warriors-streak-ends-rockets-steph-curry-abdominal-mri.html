Steph Grabs His Side, the Streak Dies in Houston, and Now We Wait on an MRI

Eight straight wins, gone in Houston. I'd trade every one of them back right now for one good phone call from the training room.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · Warriors

I knew. I didn't want to know, but I knew.

Steph hit a three about four and a half minutes into the game Wednesday night, and I did the little fist pump I do on the couch, and then I watched him hit the floor again and again and keep reaching for his right side. Every time he got up a little slower. Then a collision near the rim with 6:25 left in the first quarter, and he was walking back to the locker room with the trainer, holding his side, and my group chat went dead silent. Nobody typed anything for a solid five minutes. That's never happened. Those guys argue about everything.

The Warriors called it right abdominal soreness and ruled him out early in the second quarter. He left the arena during the second half to get an MRI. Rockets 115, Warriors 108. Eight in a row is over.

No, it wasn't a coincidence

People are going to say we just ran into a good team on the road. Houston's good, I'll give them that, they're 9 and 3 and they've got Sengun and Durant and a bunch of long, mean athletes. Fine. But you're never going to convince me the streak ending on the exact night Steph limped off five and a half minutes in was some random bounce of the basketball gods.

Look at who was already out before the tip. Jimmy Butler. Moses Moody. Gary Payton II. Brandon Williams, who hurt his hamstring Monday against the Pelicans. Porzingis isn't even with the team. Steph was the one thing holding the whole roof up, and the second he went to the locker room the roof came down. We were down 19 to 10 when he left. We were down 35 to 20 after one, and Fred VanVleet hit a three at the buzzer just to twist the knife.

And the guys still fought. That's the part that's killing me, honestly. Yaxel Lendeborg scored 13 in the second quarter all by himself and dragged us within one at 49 to 48. A rookie! Going right at Kevin Durant like he'd been doing it for ten years. We were only down five at the half. Niang and Melton opened the fourth with threes, we got it to 88 to 85, and for a few minutes I let myself believe.

Then it was 96 to 91 with seven minutes left, and Houston went on a 15 to 2 run, and Reed Sheppard hit two threes and VanVleet hit two more and it was 111 to 93 and I turned it off. Didn't even check the final until later. You know the stretch I'm talking about. The one where your best player comes off a screen and stops the bleeding with one shot. He wasn't there. That's the whole game for me.

"Concern is high, but I have no idea the extent of the injury."

Steve Kerr, after the loss in Houston

The word I can't stop thinking about

Kerr said Steph told him he tweaked something in his abdominal area and that he'd never done it before.

Never done it before. He's 38. He's in his 18th season. He's had every ankle, every knee, every shoulder this sport can throw at a body, and this is the one that's new to him? I'm not a doctor, and I'm not going to play one on a blog, but new scares me. New means nobody knows the timeline yet. New means the team's going to be careful, and they should be, and careful means games.

What we know tonight

  • Right abdominal soreness, ruled out early in the second quarter
  • He left the arena for an MRI during the second half
  • Results weren't back when Kerr talked
  • He says it's something he's never felt before

What we don't

  • How bad it actually is
  • Whether he plays Friday in San Antonio
  • Whether he finishes this road trip at all
  • Any kind of timeline, because there isn't one yet

Give the kid his flowers anyway

Lendeborg had 25 and 10 with two steals, 18 of it in the first half, the most of anybody on the floor at the break. Brandin Podziemski gave us 22 with six boards and four assists. Melton had 11. Those guys deserved a better night than the one they got.

Some other guys, less so. Al Horford made his first start of the season and went 1 for 7. Gui Santos had four turnovers and zero points in 12 minutes. Houston beat us 50 to 34 in the paint and Sengun nearly had a triple double, 19, 13 and 9. Oh, and Marcus Smart got himself tossed for punching Charles Bassey, which, sure, Marcus. Very on brand. His team still won, though, so I don't get to enjoy that one nearly as much as I want to.

Please, Steph. Please.

I keep thinking about Saturday. Forty one against the Magic, ten for ten at the line, that floater in traffic, 12 points in the last two and a half minutes of regulation like he was 27 again. Then 138 to 87 over the Pelicans on Monday and the whole Bay was walking around like we'd found a cheat code. Four days. That's how fast this sport flips on you.

We started this season 0 and 3 and he dragged us all the way to 8 and 3. We're 8 and 4 now, and one loss in Houston doesn't bother me. The road trip does. San Antonio on Friday, then New Orleans, Dallas and Memphis before we're home for Boston on the 22nd, and I'm sitting here trying to picture all of it without him and I can't.

Mostly I'm worried about him. Not the standings. Him. He's given this town everything for 18 seasons, he looked genuinely happy out there this month, and I want that back. I want the shimmy back. I want him jogging back down the court with his finger up before the ball's even through the net.

So we wait. My phone's on loud. I've refreshed the Warriors' page more times tonight than I'd like to admit, and I'll be doing it again before my coffee tomorrow. If you catch me at work staring at my phone under the table, mind your business.

Get well, Steph. We need you. We really, really need you.
