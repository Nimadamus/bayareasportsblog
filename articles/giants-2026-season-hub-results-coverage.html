The 2026 Giants Season, Game by Game, With Every Column We Wrote About It

A season that started with hope, collapsed in July and got sold off in August. The landing page for all of it, in order.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · Giants

This is the page to start from if you want the season as a story rather than as a pile of separate columns. It gets updated as games are played.

The shape of it. The Giants spent the first half hanging around, arrived at the All-Star break at 41-55, and then got worse. By the deadline they were nineteen games under .500 and selling. That is the arc, and every individual game below is a variation on it.

July: the month it ended. The bullpen defined it. Caleb Kilian coughed up a two-out lead to the worst team in baseball, and that game turned out to be the template. There was a genuinely good stretch at the end of the month - our column on the hot streak covers the run where they beat the best team in baseball twice and hung sixteen on Milwaukee - but it was a mirage inside a losing season.

The All-Star break. The first-half breakdown is the accounting: 41-55, a lost first half, and a rookie manager learning on the job in public.

August: the selloff. Three days in San Diego where they got swept, and then the deadline. The deadline column was the argument beforehand; the state of the rebuild is where things actually stand now. And then the eight-nothing loss to Detroit on 8 August, where Landen Roupp waved Tony Vitello off the mound in front of a full ballpark - the clearest picture yet of where this clubhouse stands on its manager. The day after that, Logan Webb threw eight innings without an earned run and lost anyway in ten innings, which is the other half of the same problem. Then on 11 August, Carson Whisenhunt outpitched Hunter Brown in a four-one win for win number fifty, which is the first genuinely encouraging night since the deadline.

What is still worth watching. Devers has been quietly excellent. Bryce Eldridge is the reason to keep the television on. And there is the ordinary, stubborn pleasure of watching a bad team occasionally beat a good one, which is most of what baseball is.

The games that actually explain the season. Four of them, in order. The Cease near-no-hitter, where Dylan Cease nearly no-hit one of the worst Giants lineups anyone here can remember, and which set the tone in early July. The Rockies bullpen meltdown, the template for every bullpen collapse that followed. The Padres sweep in San Diego, three days that turned the deadline from a question into a decision. And the Rangers shutout, eleven men left on base, nineteen games under, which was the moment even the optimists stopped.

The counter-programming. It was not all misery, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. Willy Adames opened the second half with a grand slam and a shutout in Seattle. There was a stretch at the end of July where this team beat the best club in baseball twice and put sixteen on Milwaukee. Rafael Devers has been quietly excellent all year. A bad season still contains good baseball; it just does not add up to anything.

What a season like this is actually for. Information. Which young arms can hold a rotation spot, whether Eldridge is what the scouting said, whether the manager can manage. Those answers are worth more in a lost year than four extra wins would have been, which is the one genuinely consoling thing about August.

How this page works. Recaps go up after games, columns go up when something deserves an argument, and both get linked here so the season reads in sequence. The roster is on the depth chart page, and everything else is on the Giants hub.
