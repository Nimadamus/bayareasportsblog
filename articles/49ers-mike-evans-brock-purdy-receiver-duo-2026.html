Mike Evans and Brock Purdy, the San Francisco 49ers Receiver Pairing Nobody in This Building Has Ever Had Before

Six foot five. Ten years in the league. A catch radius that has made grown cornerbacks look silly since he was a rookie in Tampa. Purdy has thrown to a Hall of Fame tight end and a running back who breaks the internet weekly. He has never thrown to this.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · 49ers

I've watched every snap of Brock Purdy's career, and I keep coming back to the same thought this camp. He has had a Hall of Fame tight end. He has had the most explosive back in football when that back is upright. What he has never had, not once, is a receiver built the way Mike Evans is built, and now he has him, and I'm not going to pretend that isn't the thing I think about the second I close my eyes at night.

Ten straight thousand yard seasons. Ten. Nobody in the history of this league has ever opened a career like that, and Evans did it in Tampa against every kind of coverage a defense could throw at him, mostly without the benefit of a run game that made anybody's life easier. Now he's ours, and he's lining up across from a quarterback who has a career passer rating better than Aaron Rodgers ever posted, and I keep having to remind myself this is a real sentence and not something I made up in a fever dream during the offseason.

Purdy has thrown to a rebuilt receiver room this summer, and rebuilt doesn't begin to cover it. Deebo came home. Christian Kirk arrived. De'Zhaun Stribling has been the story of camp as a rookie. Ricky Pearsall, brutally, is out for the year before he got to show what he could do next to all of it. But Evans is the one who changes the math on every single snap, because he isn't a slot receiver winning on quickness or a possession guy moving the chains underneath. He's a six foot five contested catch machine who has been beating grown men at the catch point since before some of this roster could drive, and Purdy has never had that particular weapon in his toolbox.

Think about what that actually means on a Sunday. Red zone fade route, one on one, ball placed high and outside where only Evans can go get it. That's a call Kyle Shanahan hasn't had in his pocket in San Francisco, not really, not with a receiver this size and this proven. George Kittle wins in the middle of the field. McCaffrey wins with the ball in his hands in space. Evans wins in the air, above the defender, on third and seven when everybody in the building knows the ball is coming his way and he still comes down with it anyway. That's a different kind of weapon than this offense has carried, and pairing it with a quarterback who has this much accuracy and this much command of the offense is exactly the sort of thing that keeps Niner Nation up at night in the good way for once.

I know what the skeptics are going to say, because I'm usually one of them. New system, new quarterback, a receiver room that has already had two full rebuilds in one summer, and Evans isn't getting any younger. Fair. I've lived through enough hyped up additions in this building to know that chemistry takes time and camp buzz doesn't always survive September. But this isn't a projection built on hope. This is a proven, decade long producer walking into an offense with one of the best play callers alive and a quarterback who's playing the best football of his life, and those two things don't usually miss each other by much.

The rest of this offense is loaded around them, hurt as it's in spots, and the full picture of who's healthy and who isn't lives on the depth chart page. The season opens against the Rams at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, which is still the strangest sentence in the Australia trip, and the whole slate is laid out on the season schedule hub. Everything else on this team lives on the 49ers hub.

I've tried to talk myself down from this all camp and I can't do it. Purdy has never had a receiver like Mike Evans. Now he does. If this works the way it looks like it should on paper, defenses are about to have a very bad year trying to figure out who to take away, and I for one am not going to spend the next month pretending I'm not excited about it.
