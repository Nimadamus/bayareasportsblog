Niners vs. Raiders Is Back in the Bay, and It Still Means Something

The Raiders play a real game in the Bay Area today for the first time since they packed up for Las Vegas. They left. Half my family didn't. That's why this one matters.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · 49ers

My uncle called me Saturday night. He never calls. He texts in all caps and sends pictures of his dog in a Raiders bandana. But he called, and the first thing he said was, "You going?"

No hello. Just that.

He's lived in San Leandro his whole life. He had season tickets at the Coliseum for longer than I've been alive, he cried in his truck the day they played their last game there, and he swore he'd never give Mark Davis another dollar. He's driving down to Santa Clara today with two of his buddies, all three of them in silver and black, and he's paying Levi's parking to watch his team play mine.

I love him. I want the Niners to beat his team by 30.

The facts, before I get emotional

Raiders at Niners, 1:25 this afternoon at Levi's, on FOX. We're 6 and 1, coming off the bye, and we're 4 and 0 at home. They're 4 and 4 after snapping a four game losing streak by outlasting the Jets 38 to 33 last week. The Niners are around 10 point favorites, which my uncle calls "disrespectful" and I call "about right."

And this is the first regular season game the Raiders have played anywhere in the Bay Area since they moved to Las Vegas in 2020. The last time they came to Levi's for one that counted was November of 2018, a Thursday night, and Nick Mullens of all people threw for three touchdowns in his first start and we won 34 to 3. Kittle took one up the seam and just kept going. I still have that clip saved on my phone.

How we got here

1960

The Raiders start in Oakland. For a long time this whole region had two teams, two fan bases, and a lot of Thanksgiving dinners that ended badly.

1982

Al Davis takes them to Los Angeles. My uncle doesn't talk about the 80s.

1995

They come home. Mount Davis goes up, the Black Hole gets loud, and the Battle of the Bay preseason games get so nasty that after the Candlestick parking lot shootings in 2011 they got shelved for years.

2019

Last game at the Coliseum, a December loss to Jacksonville. Players walking over to the stands afterward to say goodbye. I wasn't even a Raiders fan and I felt it.

2020

Las Vegas. Gone.

Today

Back in the Bay for a game that counts, as the road team. In Santa Clara. Against us.

Why it still matters here

People outside the Bay never get this part. The Raiders moved. The fans mostly didn't.

Go to any taco truck in East Oakland on a Sunday, any bar in Hayward, any backyard in Vallejo or Antioch, and you'll still see the shield everywhere. Those people didn't stop being Raiders fans because a billionaire found a better stadium deal. They just stopped getting to go to games without a plane ticket. Today they get to drive. Some of them haven't seen their team in person in years.

I'm going to be honest, as a Niners fan, that makes me a little nervous about the crowd. Levi's has been taken over by visiting fans before, and we all remember how that felt. If there's a lot of silver and black in those seats today, and I think there will be, I don't want to hear one word about it from anybody in red. Show up. Be loud. It's our building.

But I also kind of love it. This region feels more like itself when both of these teams are in the same building. I didn't realize I'd miss hating the Raiders up close until they were gone.

Why I'm nervous

  • Kirk Cousins has 19 touchdowns and five picks. He's been way better than anybody expected.
  • Brock Bowers. Nobody on our side has figured out how to cover a tight end like that for four quarters.
  • Maxx Crosby lives for a game like this, and it'll feel like a home game for him.
  • We turned it over four times against Atlanta before the bye.

Why I'm not

  • Purdy has 15 touchdowns, four picks and a 110.1 rating, and he's only been sacked seven times.
  • The Raiders have given up 27 or more in six straight games.
  • McCaffrey and Bosa are both off the injury report.
  • We're 4 and 0 at home. This is the fifth.

The matchups I'm watching

Bosa, first. He missed five games with the calf and he's finally off the report. I'm trying not to get excited. I'm failing. Put him and DeForest Buckner on the same line, Buckner back in red after all those years in Indy, and I want to see how long Cousins gets to stand back there. Cousins isn't a guy who runs around.

And there's a weird little story with Cousins too. His first offensive coordinator in the NFL was Kyle Shanahan, back in Washington. Kyle knows him about as well as anybody. I'd bet a burrito he's got a couple of things drawn up just for him.

On our side, we're thin at receiver. Deebo's out with the hamstring, Mike Evans is out with the Achilles, and Christian Kirk is on IR. So it's going to be Kittle, McCaffrey and whoever Kyle can scheme open. Kittle already has 425 yards and five touchdowns. Feed him. It's a Raiders game. He knows what to do.

The guy on their side who scares me most is Crosby. Colton McKivitz hasn't given up a sack all year, and today's going to be his hardest day of the season. If McKivitz holds up, we win this game. I really believe that.

What I want from today

I want a win. Obviously. But I want it to look like 2018, not like a game where we sweat it out in the fourth quarter. The Raiders defense is giving up points to everybody. We have no excuse to make this close.

And I want my uncle to go home quiet. Not sad. Just quiet. I want him to text me in all caps tonight, something like "LUCKY," and I want to send him the final score and nothing else.

He'll still come to Thanksgiving. He'll still bring the bandana dog. That's the Bay. One family, two teams, and now one of them flies in.
