Tigers 11, A's 0: Swept, Shut Out, and Outscored 32-7 in Three Days

Thirteen to one on Friday. Eight to six on Saturday. Eleven to nothing on Sunday. Detroit came to Sacramento, took everything that was not nailed down, and left with a sweep so lopsided the A's had to use a position player to finish the last inning.

The series was over before it started, and then it kept going

Detroit scored thirteen on Friday. Eight on Saturday. Eleven on Sunday, and the A's did not put a single run on the board in the finale. Thirty-two to seven across a weekend at that ballpark in Sacramento, in front of the crowd this ownership group earned. If you are looking for a single set of numbers that explains what this franchise has become since it walked out on Oakland, that is the set.

Gage Jump got the ball and did not survive the fourth: 3.2 innings, four hits, six runs, six walks. Six walks. He struck out five, so it is not as if he had nothing, but you cannot put that many free runners on against a lineup that is fighting for a wild card. Spencer Torkelson doubled home two in the first before the A's had swung the bat in anger. In the fourth, Gleyber Torres singled home two more and Eduardo Valencia doubled home two after that, and it was 6-0 with more than half the game left to play.

Keider Montero made them look like a Triple-A club

Six innings, two hits, no runs, two walks, two strikeouts. That was Montero's whole afternoon. The A's did not get a runner past second against him in any meaningful way and the offense never adjusted. They finished with seven hits and nine men left on base and zero runs, which is its own special kind of incompetence — you have to actually reach base repeatedly to strand nine.

Jeff McNeil had two of the seven hits. Tyler Soderstrom, Lawrence Butler, Jonah Heim, Brian Serven and Alika Williams had one apiece. Nobody drove in a run because nobody was ever on base at the same time as anybody else. Henry Bolte went 0-for-3 and spent his afternoon chasing line drives to center field, which is at least honest work.

Then the eighth inning happened

Down 6-0 in the eighth, out came Ben Bowden, and Detroit hung four on him in an inning: Valencia's second two-run double of the day, a Torkelson RBI single, a force-out that scored another. Ten to nothing. Valencia finished with four RBIs. Torkelson with three.

And then the truly humiliating part. Carlos Cortes — an outfielder, a guy who had already taken two at-bats in this game — went out to pitch the ninth, because there was no reason left to burn a real arm. James Outman hit his fifth home run of the year off him. Eleven to nothing. That is where this season is: your position player mopping up a blowout in your own ballpark and still giving up a homer.

Five straight, and it is going to get worse

Two losses to Boston to close out last week, three to Detroit this weekend. Five in a row. Forty-five and sixty-seven, twenty-two games under .500, fourth in the AL West, twelve and a half back of a division they were leading earlier this year. Remember that? Remember the A's being in first place and everyone in Sacramento acting like the move had been vindicated? That was the mirage, and I said so at the break, and there is no joy in watching the collapse arrive right on schedule.

They hit .264 at home and .225 on the road, they cannot get length out of a starter, and the trade deadline is Monday, which means whatever is left of value on this roster is about to be somebody else's. Cincinnati is next, on the road, where they cannot hit.

This is not our team anymore. It has not been for a while. But the guys wearing the uniform still have to go out there every day and get beat 11-0 in a minor league park because a billionaire decided a real city was not worth keeping, and that is worth saying out loud every single time it happens.
