Athletics 4, Astros 3: The Fiftieth Win Arrived in Houston on a Wild Pitch and a Ground Ball, and Jacob Lopez Deserved a Better Team Than This One

Nine strikeouts from Jacob Lopez, a lead handed straight back in the seventh, and then a rally in the eighth where the two biggest runs scored without anybody hitting the ball out of the infield. Fifty wins on the twenty second of August.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · Athletics

They won one. On a Saturday night in Houston, in front of a crowd that had no idea who half of them were, the Athletics finally won a baseball game. Four to three. Their fiftieth win of the season, which they collected on the twenty second of August, with a hundred and thirty games in the books.

I want to be nice about it. I genuinely do. And then I look at how it happened and I can't stop laughing, and it isn't a good laugh.

Jacob Lopez was magnificent, and I need everybody to stop for a second and register that. Six innings. Three hits. One run. One walk. Nine strikeouts. In Houston, against a lineup with Yordan Alvarez and Jeremy Peña and Isaac Paredes in it. He walked out there for a team that's thirty games under .500, in a season that ended for them sometime around the second week of July, and he carved up a .500 ball club for two hours like it mattered.

That's the fourth or fifth time this summer I've written a version of that paragraph. He shut out the Twins in July. He struck out nine on eighty seven pitches against Cincinnati and Mark Kotsay took him out anyway and the bullpen lost it and the streak went to seven. The man has spent an entire season pitching well for a franchise that can't hold a lead, can't hit, can't field, and doesn't have a home. He's going to end up with an ERA that looks nothing like his record and nobody outside of about four thousand people is ever going to know.

Walton put them ahead in the third with a solo home run to dead center that went four hundred and twenty two feet, which in a season this bleak counts as a genuinely nice thing to see. One nothing. Tommy White had three hits on the night, Butler had two, McNeil had two, and they piled up ten hits total, which for this offense is practically a parade.

And then the seventh inning happened, because with this team the seventh or eighth inning always happens. Luis Medina came in for Lopez and Daulton Varsho hit the second pitch he liked into the left field seats to put Houston ahead. Then Wade Jr. doubled to center. Then Jeremy Peña singled him in. Two to one became three to one, the lead was gone, and every single person who has watched this club since June knew precisely what came next.

Except it didn't come next. That's the twist. For once, it didn't.

The eighth inning was the strangest rally I've watched all year. Sousa came out for Houston and immediately lost the plot. McNeil singled to right. Gelof walked. A sacrifice bunt moved them both up. And then Sousa uncorked a wild pitch, McNeil trotted home, Gelof went to third, and just like that the deficit was one and nobody in an Athletics uniform had earned a thing.

Butler singled to right to score Gelof and tie it, which was an actual base hit and deserves credit. Bryan Abreu came in. Tommy White doubled to left. And the run that won this baseball game, the fourth run, the one that put them in front, scored on a Heim ground ball to shortstop.

Read that back. The tying run scored on a wild pitch. The winning run scored on a ground out to short. Their fiftieth victory of the 2026 season was manufactured almost entirely out of another team's mistakes, which, when you think about what this organization has become, is the most perfectly fitting thing that could possibly have happened.

Harris threw a clean eighth. Alvarado struck out two in the ninth and shut the door. The bullpen, which has spent this entire summer setting fire to leads in Kansas City and Kansas City again, held a one run lead for six outs. That's the headline. Six outs. That's where the bar is now.

Fifty and eighty. Thirty games under .500. Twenty seven and forty on the road. They come into this game having been swept four straight in Kansas City and then shut out four to nothing by these same Astros on Friday night, so this is one win at the end of a stretch that had gotten genuinely hard to look at.

And here's the thing I keep coming back to, and I'm going to keep coming back to it as long as this site exists. Nobody in the Bay Area got to see this. Not the Walton home run, not the nine strikeouts, not the weird little eighth inning that ended in a win. A Saturday night baseball game played by a team called the Athletics in a stadium in Texas, watched by almost nobody, on the way back to a minor league park in West Sacramento that they're pretending is a major league home. We had this. We had a hundred years of it. And it got moved out from under a city that loved it by an owner who didn't.

So no, I'm not going to write a happy recap. Jacob Lopez was excellent and he deserves better than the uniform he's wearing and the situation he's pitching in. Walton hit a ball four hundred and twenty two feet and that was fun for four seconds. Tommy White had three hits and looks like a real hitter. Those are three good things and I mean all three of them.

But it's the twenty second of August, they've fifty wins, they've thirty two games left, and the whole thing is being played out in front of a fan base that had to be surgically removed from the team first. I called the hot start a mirage at the break and the free fall never actually stopped, and now we're just counting down.

They won one. Enjoy it. That's genuinely the whole review. The roster is on the depth chart page, the ballpark situation is laid out in the Sutter Health Park guide, and the rest of this sad little season is on the A's page.
