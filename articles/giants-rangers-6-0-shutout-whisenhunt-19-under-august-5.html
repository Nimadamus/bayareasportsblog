Rangers 6, Giants 0: Eleven Men Left On Base, Zero for Eight With Runners in Scoring Position, and Nineteen Games Under .500

The offense got eleven men on base in scoring position situations and scored nobody. NOBODY. Zero for eight. Shut out by a pitcher who had not started a big-league game since September of 2024. And now this team is nineteen games under .500 for the first time all year, which means the floor everybody swore we had hit in July was not the floor.

Zero for eight. Say it out loud.

I want to be careful here because I have written a version of this column eleven times since June and I am running out of ways to say the same thing without repeating myself. But Wednesday night in Arlington was a different flavor of miserable, and the reason is that the Giants were not overmatched. They were not no-hit. They put ELEVEN men on base and stranded every single one of them. Zero for eight with runners in scoring position. Eight separate times, a Giants hitter came to the plate with a man on second or third and a chance to change the temperature of the game, and eight separate times, nothing. A ground ball, a strikeout, a lazy fly, a rally that evaporates before you have finished getting off the couch.

That is the whole season in one box score line. This team is not talentless. It gets runners on. It is 48-67 because the at-bat that actually costs something is the one nobody on this roster can win right now, and that has been true since April, and the front office watched the deadline come and go and decided the answer was to subtract instead of add.

The game was over before the fourth inning started

Carson Whisenhunt gave up all six. The third inning was the killer — five of the first six men he faced the second time through reached base, Justin Foscue laced a two-run triple into the gap, and by the time the dust settled it was a four-run inning and a 6-0 game with two-thirds of the night still to play. Brandon Nimmo added a two-run day of his own, including a home run he sliced just fair down the left-field line in the fifth for good measure, because of course.

And I am not here to torch Whisenhunt. The kid threw a gem against the Rockies a few weeks ago and has had real nights this year. But there is a pattern with this rotation that has become impossible to unsee: they get through a lineup once looking sharp, and the second time around the whole thing collapses in the space of four batters. It happened to Roupp in San Diego. It happened here. Nobody on the staff seems capable of surviving a third inning where the other team has seen the stuff already.

Shut out by a guy who had not pitched since 2024

Here is the part that will keep me up. Texas handed the ball to Cody Bradford, who had not appeared in a big-league game since September 2024 and had elbow surgery in June of last year. First time back. Rusty. Seventy pitches, four and a third innings, five hits and two walks allowed — he was NOT sharp, he put traffic on the bases all night, and he still walked off with a scoreless line because the Giants could not push a single runner across against a man making his first start in twenty-three months.

That is the eleventh time this team has been shut out this season. Eleven. And it is August 5th.

Nineteen games under, and the number that actually matters

48-67. Twenty-two and forty on the road. Nineteen games under .500 for the first time this season, which is the kind of milestone a franchise passes on the way down and never announces. Every few weeks somebody in the organization talks about the young core and the second half and the foundation, and every few weeks the record gets worse. There is no more talking around it. This is a bad baseball team having a bad baseball season and the deadline was the last chance to signal that anybody upstairs understood the size of the problem. They did not.

What is left is exactly what I said after Tuesday's walk-off: give the young guys everything. Eldridge every day. McCray, Gilbert, Basabe, every single at-bat that exists between now and October. There is no lineup construction left to protect. There is nothing to lose because it has already been lost. Play the kids, find out who is real, and stop asking us to be patient about a plan that keeps producing 0-for-8 with runners in scoring position.

Go Giants. I mean it, even now. But somebody has to hit with a man on second.
