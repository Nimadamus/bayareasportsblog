Giants 3, Brewers 0: Tyler Mahle and Grant McCray Shut the Best Team in Baseball Down

The Brewers came into Oracle Park with 66 wins and the best record in the sport. For one night, none of that mattered. Tyler Mahle carved through Milwaukee's lineup for six shutout innings, Grant McCray put a fourth-inning fastball into the seats, and Erik Miller slammed the door in the ninth. Giants win 3-0. San Francisco improves to 45-61.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · Giants Column

Nobody circled this one as a signature win when the schedule came out, mostly because nobody expected the Giants to still be looking for signature wins in late July. But there it was: the 66-40 Brewers, the best record in baseball, held to three hits and zero runs over nine innings by a Giants team that has spent most of this season finding new ways to lose close ones. Tonight they just won, cleanly, and it did not need extra innings or a ninth-inning miracle to get there.

Tyler Mahle was the story for six innings before McCray ever touched a bat. He worked around traffic all night without ever really being in trouble, six innings, three hits, zero runs, two walks and three strikeouts, the kind of start that does not jump off the box score but wins a series opener against a 66-win team. He handed a lead to the bullpen instead of a mess, and Erik Miller finished it in the ninth with a perfect inning and two strikeouts for his third save. In between, nothing from Milwaukee's lineup ever threatened to matter.

The offense did not need much, and it did not give much beyond two swings. Luis Arraez singled through the right side in the third to score J. Rodriguez, who had doubled, and that was 1-0. Grant McCray made sure it did not stay a one-run game, turning on a pitch in the fourth and driving it 406 feet for a two-run homer that put the Giants up 3-0 for good. Brandon Sproat took the loss for Milwaukee despite battling through six innings himself, six hits and three earned runs the difference between a competitive start and a bad one.

Casey Schmitt left the game early with what looked like a leg injury, and that is the one thing tempering an otherwise clean night — the Giants have not had many nights this clean, and they cannot afford to lose a piece off this roster while they are still fighting to find their footing. For now, take the win. Beating the best team in baseball with a shutout, on a night the offense needed exactly two hits to matter, is not something this team has done often enough to take for granted. Giants improve to 45-61 and get to actually enjoy a getaway day for once.
