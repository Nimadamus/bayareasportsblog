Steph Curry Has a Torn Oblique, He's Out at Least Two Weeks, and I Counted Every Game

Two weeks is when they look at him again. Nobody said it's when he plays again. I got out the calendar anyway.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · Warriors

Torn oblique. Right side. That's what the MRI said.

The Warriors put it out Thursday and I read it standing in my kitchen with the coffee still dripping, and I had to read it twice, because the two words everybody grabbed were "two weeks" and that isn't really what it says. It says he'll be re-evaluated in two weeks. Checked again. Nobody promised me a basketball game on the other side of that checkup, and the people who cover this team every day are already whispering that it's closer to a month.

So I did what I always do when this team makes me nervous. I got out the schedule and a pen.

What actually happened to him

Go back to Wednesday in Houston. About four and a half minutes in, 7:22 on the clock, Steph comes off a Draymond screen and buries one from 28 feet. Normal stuff for him. Except he yelled when he let it go, and then he waved at the bench. He'd already been grabbing at that right side for a few possessions. He was in the locker room with 6:25 left in the first quarter. Six minutes, three points, and that was his night.

There's been a lot of noise about Alperen Sengun getting tangled up with his arm a little earlier, and I must've watched that clip forty times looking for somebody to be mad at. Kerr watched it too and said he didn't think there was anything dirty about it. Fine. I'm still not sending Sengun a Christmas card.

Kerr also said that when he saw the tape, he wished he'd called a timeout quicker. You and me both, Steve. The man was holding his side and looking over at the trainers, and we let him run off one more screen.

Why this one scares me more than an ankle

Your oblique is the muscle that wraps around your side and lets you twist. Now think about what a Steph Curry jumper actually is. Catch, turn, rise, and that snap through the middle of his body that gets the ball gone before the defender's hand is even up. He does it hundreds of times a day. You can tape an ankle. You can't tape a twist.

It's mostly a baseball injury, too, and if you've suffered through enough Giants summers you already know how those go. A guy swings, grabs his side, says he feels great ten days later, takes one hack in the cage too early and disappears for another month. Basketball players almost never tear one. That's probably why Steph told Kerr it was something he'd never felt before. He's 38, he's in his 18th season, and his body just found a brand new way to ruin my week.

So when I hear the Warriors are bracing for a month, I don't even get mad about it. Good. Take the month! The first week or so is nothing but rest anyway, and I don't want him anywhere near a basketball until he can sneeze without wincing.

What we just lost

27.8points a night
45.7%from three, on pace for his best ever
75 of 76at the free throw line

He was playing some of the best basketball of his life, and I'm not saying that to be dramatic. Forty four in Minnesota. Forty one against Orlando last Saturday. One missed free throw all season. One! We lost our first three games, then we won eight in a row, and he was on the floor for seven of those eight.

That's what's sitting in a training room right now.

Friday night was the preview, and I hated it

Spurs123
Friday in San Antonio
Warriors85

First game without him. It was 38 to 14 after one quarter.

We missed our first 11 shots. Eleven. It was 18 to 0 before anybody in a Warriors jersey scored, and I was eating dinner in front of it and I just put the fork down.

The five starters combined for 30 points on 10 of 43 shooting. Yaxel Lendeborg, who's been the best story of this whole season, went 1 for 13 and finished with three points. Podziemski was 2 for 10, and De'Anthony Melton, starting in Steph's spot, wasn't any better. San Antonio beat us 60 to 43 on the glass and Stephon Castle got 32 like he was in a layup line. The only guy who looked like he wanted the ball was LJ Cryer, who came off the bench and hit six threes for 20.

I'm not burying the kid over one night. Yaxel had 25 and 10 in Houston two days earlier with the whole building coming down around him. But this is what it looks like when Steph's in street clothes: every defense in the league gets to load up on a rookie and dare everybody else to make a shot. The Spurs are 11 and 2. They smelled it in about ninety seconds.

The calendar on my fridge

We're 8 and 5 this morning, seventh in the West, three games out of first. He's out for at least the next seven, and one of them's already gone. Here's the rest, with all times Pacific.

The Mavs are 7 and 5 and breathing right down our necks in the standings. I think this is the swing game of the whole trip. Win it and I can relax a little. Lose it and I start doing bad math.

At Portland

The one piece of luck in all of this

Say it does turn into a month. Look at what comes after Portland. The Clippers at Chase on the 29th, Utah at Chase on December 1, and then, the way the schedule sits today, there's nothing on it for us until December 12 in Cleveland. Some of that hole gets filled in once the NBA Cup shakes out. It's still the softest stretch on the whole calendar, and it shows up right when a 38 year old with a torn muscle in his side would need it most.

A month from the night he got hurt is December 11.

So that's the math I've talked myself into. He misses nine, maybe a couple more, and he walks back out there in Cleveland with a full month of rest in him. I'd sign for that right now, in blood, on the fridge.

Somebody has to be good until then

Jimmy Butler's still out after surgery on his right knee. Moses Moody's working his way back from surgery on his left one. Brandon Williams has the hamstring, and Porzingis isn't with the team. That's a lot of salary in nice sweaters.

So it's Podziemski's team for a while. He had 22 in Houston and I need that guy, not Friday's guy. It's Yaxel's team too, rookie or not. And it's on Draymond to make sure San Antonio was one ugly night and not the start of something, because I remember last year. We went 13 and 26 in the games Steph missed. I watched all 26. We're 1 and 2 without him this season, and the 1 was the Clippers.

The pen's still on the fridge. I crossed out San Antonio this morning, harder than I needed to. Five more before Thanksgiving, and then somebody in a team polo presses on his side and we all find out what December looks like.
