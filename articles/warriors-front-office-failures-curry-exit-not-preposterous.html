The Front Office Keeps Failing Steph Curry, and "He Could Leave" Doesn't Sound Crazy Anymore

I never thought I'd type this sentence, but here we are: the front office has run this out long enough that Steph Curry finishing his career somewhere other than Golden State is not a crazy thought anymore. It's an uncomfortable one, and it needs to be said out loud.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · Warriors Column

I want to be careful here because I love this team and I am not one of those fans who turns on the franchise the second a season goes sideways. But I also can't keep pretending I don't see what's happening. For years, the front office treated Steph Curry's greatness like a renewable resource, something that would just keep generating contention no matter what got built, or not built, around it. That was never true. It was true enough, for long enough, that it started to feel true. It isn't anymore, and the gap between what this front office has delivered and what Steph has earned is the story nobody wants to write.

Start with the draft capital, because that's where this really begins. Golden State spent years shipping firsts out the door for immediate fixes, betting over and over that the next trade would buy one more real shot instead of building anything underneath the core. Some of those swings worked out fine. Plenty didn't, and the ones that didn't left this front office with almost no young talent developed in-house to hand Steph when he needed it most. You don't get to complain about an aging roster around your franchise player when you're the one who spent a decade refusing to develop the players who'd eventually replace the guys currently aging out.

Then there's the pattern of half-measures at the trade deadline. Real contenders around the league make the uncomfortable move, the one that costs something, when their timeline demands it. This front office has made a habit of the compromise version instead: a solid rotation piece here, a reclamation project there, moves that look active without actually changing what the team is. Steph doesn't need a front office that's busy. He needs one that's decisive. Those are not the same thing, and the difference has quietly cost this team multiple postseasons that were there for the taking.

None of this is abstract to Steph, either, whatever he says publicly. He has watched cheaper, better-run organizations build sustainable rosters around one star while his own front office kept patching holes with tape. He has watched the supporting cast get older every year the front office refused to plan two years ahead. At some point, watching that happen to your prime, year after year, changes how a player thinks about loyalty. It doesn't mean he wants out. It means the idea is no longer unthinkable, and that alone is a massive failure on the front office's part, because keeping it unthinkable was the entire job.

I still believe Steph retires a Warrior. I want that more than almost anything in sports right now, and I think, deep down, he wants it too. But wanting it and the front office actually earning it are two different things, and only one of those is happening right now. If this organization keeps mistaking activity for a plan, keeps burning draft picks on band-aids, keeps handing Steph a roster that's a year behind instead of a year ahead, then "Steph Curry somewhere else" stops being a thought experiment and starts being a real conversation. That should terrify every single person running this front office. It's on them now to make sure it never gets past the thought-experiment stage.
