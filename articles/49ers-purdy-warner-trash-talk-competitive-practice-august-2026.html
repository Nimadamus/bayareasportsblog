Purdy and Warner Were Talking Trash to Each Other in Practice, and It Was the Best Thing I Have Seen All Camp

Practice 16 in Santa Clara was the loudest session of the summer. Fred Warner won a rep and let the offense hear about it. Brock Purdy came right back over the middle and let Warner hear about it. Then the two of them were laughing together a few minutes later. If you want to know why I am not worried about this football team, watch that sequence.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · 49ers

There is a very specific sound a training camp practice makes when it stops being a walkthrough and starts being a fight. Sunday afternoon in Santa Clara had that sound, and it came from the two most important players on this roster going at each other.

Here is how it happened. Fred Warner did what Fred Warner does. He blew up a rep in the middle of the field, got the better of rookie running back Kaelon Black on a pass breakup, and then he talked about it. Warner is not a screamer. He just lets you know. The offense heard every word.

Brock Purdy did not let it go.

Next chance he got, Purdy dropped a throw over the middle, past Warner, into Deebo Samuel's hands for a big gain. And then he turned and gave it right back to the linebacker in front of the whole field, loud, with a word in it that I am not going to print here. He closed the session with a touchdown throw to Demarcus Robinson, because of course he did.

That is your quiet, seventh round, Mr. Irrelevant quarterback. Talking smack to the best linebacker in football on a Sunday in August.

And then they laughed about it. That is the part everybody skipped past and it is the whole point of the story. A few minutes after the jawing, the two of them were standing there together, talking, cracking up. No sulking, no separate corners, no coach walking over to referee it.

Colton McKivitz put it perfectly when he was asked about his quarterback. He may give off quietness, McKivitz said, but no, he has got some fire in him for sure.

Thank you. Some of us have been saying that for three years while the national guys kept calling him a game manager who gets carried by the scheme.

Klay Kubiak loved it too. The offensive coordinator was asked about the two of them going at it and he said he thinks it is cool, that it is competitive, that it is respect between teammates. Guys who can talk a little smack to each other and still be dead serious about the same goal. That is not a coach spinning an incident. That is a coach who understood exactly what he had just watched.

Because here is the thing about a football team in the back half of August. Everybody is tired. Everybody is sore. The pads have been on for weeks and you are running the same install against the same faces for the tenth straight day. That is precisely when teams go soft and start jogging through periods. What Warner and Purdy did on Sunday is the opposite of that. They dragged everybody else's standard up with them for two hours.

This is what leadership actually looks like, and it is not a speech. Purdy is the leader of the offense. Warner is the leader of the defense. They are the two guys in that building whose habits set the temperature for everyone else, and neither of them decided that a Sunday in the third week of camp was a day to coast. Warner is not going half speed against a rookie running back. Purdy is not shrugging and moving on when he gets shown up.

Rookies watch that. Practice squad guys watch that. The receivers fighting for the last two spots on the roster watch that. When your quarterback and your middle linebacker refuse to lose a rep in August, nobody else on that field gets to take one off.

And you cannot fake the chemistry part. There is a version of this story that goes badly. There are locker rooms where the quarterback and the defensive captain start jawing and it turns into a real thing, cliques form, and by October somebody is talking to a reporter anonymously. That is not this. This is two guys who genuinely like each other pushing each other into being uncomfortable, because they both know that whatever they cannot handle in Santa Clara in August is going to eat them alive in January.

Warner has been the best all around linebacker in the sport for years, and he has never had to cover a quarterback this good every single day in practice. Purdy has never had a defensive player challenge him like that inside his own building. They are making each other better, and that is not a cliche when you can point at the actual reps.

The rest of the day was pretty good too. George Kittle and Christian McCaffrey were both back out there, which is the news a lot of Niner fans have been waiting on all month. Kubiak called them two Hall of Fame caliber players who practice at a high standard and lift everybody up, and you could see it in how the offense moved. Jordan James turned a corner and took off on one run that reminded everybody the room behind McCaffrey is not empty.

Purdy with a full set of weapons, in a competitive practice, against a defense that is not giving him anything for free. That is the picture you want in the last week of August.

What I take from Sunday. The 49ers open in Melbourne against the Rams, on the other side of the planet, in the strangest travel week this franchise has ever put together. You are going to need a team that does not have to be plugged in by a coach. You are going to need two or three guys who set the standard themselves and hold everybody to it whether anybody is watching or not.

On Sunday afternoon in Santa Clara, in front of a few hundred people and a handful of reporters, the quarterback and the linebacker showed you they are those guys. They fought over a rep in a practice that does not count for anything, then they laughed about it, then they went back out and did it again.

I will take that over another quiet, efficient, forgettable Sunday walkthrough every single time.
