49ers Column

They Finally Gave Brock Purdy a Fight. He Won It Going Away.

The most competitive practice of this camp by a mile. The defense finally showed up with something to say — picks, punch-outs, breakups, the whole menu — and the quarterback still walked off that field as the best player on it. Twelve of seventeen on thirty-two team snaps, throwing to guys who were unemployed a week ago.

This was the first day the defense actually punched back

Every camp has a day where somebody stops rolling over. Jack Jones tipped a ball to himself and picked it off. Renardo Green got one in one-on-ones. Ji'Ayir Brown punched a ball out and Green fell on it. Upton Stout broke up a pass and looked like the best second-year player on this roster doing it. Luke Gifford read a route perfectly and had an interception in his hands before he had to settle for the breakup. That is a real defensive afternoon. That is not a walkthrough where the offense gets to feel good about itself.

And Brock Purdy went twelve for seventeen on thirty-two snaps in team periods and ran the entire operation anyway. That is the point. It is easy to complete passes when the defense is on a maintenance plan and nobody is disguising anything. Thursday they came after him and he was still the calmest, fastest-thinking human on that grass.

He was throwing to guys who were on their couch last week

Look at who caught the football. Malik Turner — a thirty-year-old undrafted receiver most of you had to Google — caught all three balls thrown his way, including a deep shot from Purdy that got the crowd out of their seats. KhaDarel Hodge, signed days ago, immediately ran under a deep ball from him. Deebo got a package of designed touches, handoffs and pitches and catches, because the receiver room is still a hospital ward.

That is a quarterback making replacement-level bodies look like an offense. Every year we have this stupid argument about whether Purdy is a system product. Go watch him make a street free agent look like a starter on a day where the defense was winning reps. There is your answer. He does not need the machine. The machine has been needing him.

The Layer King

Raheem Morris — a man who has been a head coach, who has coached against real quarterbacks, who has no reason to hand out compliments in August — started calling him the Layer King. Because Purdy throws into the layers of a defense, the little windows between levels that most quarterbacks never even see, and he does it on time and on the correct shoulder.

And then Morris said the part I actually loved. He got fired up. He said Purdy got competitive out there, that he had some intensity to him, and that when he does that, it makes the whole team go. That is the thing people from outside the Bay never understood about this guy. They sold him as the nice, humble, aw-shucks last pick in the draft. He is a stone-cold competitive maniac who happens to be polite about it. Thursday he let it show, on a day when the defense was actually testing him, and the entire field picked up.

Doing it on 87 Day, which is the part that gets me

This all happened on 87 Day, the day the franchise stops and honors Dwight Clark, raises money and awareness for ALS, and brings the old guys back. Brent Jones was there. Bryant Young. Ronnie Lott. Those men are standing on the sideline watching a quarterback drop dimes into windows the size of a mailbox and rally a young roster on a hard day of work.

I do not need to make that mean more than it does. But if you grew up in this thing, you know what it feels like when the past and the present are standing on the same grass and the present is holding up its end.

What I am still not calm about

The list. Always the list. Nick Bosa missed a second straight day. Dre Greenlaw was limited. Mike Evans, Christian Kirk, Kyle Juszczyk, Jordan James, Vederian Lowe, Keion White, Malik Mustapha, Jacob Cowing, Carver Willis, Nick Zakelj, Brett Toth — all out. Sam Okuayinonu, Osa Odighizuwa and De'Zhaun Stribling got back on the field, which counts for something, and Gracen Halton came back off the knee bruise and got interior push right away.

But they signed another offensive lineman, Kion Smith, and waived Colton Dowell, which is the kind of transaction you make when the training room is dictating the roster. Four straight Augusts of this. I am supposed to believe the fifth one ends differently.

What it means

It means the one thing you cannot buy, cannot sign off the street, cannot rehab and cannot scheme around is already handled. The quarterback is playing the best football of his life, on the hardest day of camp, against a defense that came to work, with a receiver corps assembled from waiver claims. That is not a summer mirage. That is a guy raising his floor while everybody around him is on a bike.

They walked through Friday and go again Saturday morning. Get healthy. He is doing his part.
