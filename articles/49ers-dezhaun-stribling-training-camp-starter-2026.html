De'Zhaun Stribling Keeps Balling Out in Camp, and a Starting Job Is Starting to Look Inevitable

This isn't a one-good-practice story anymore. De'Zhaun Stribling has strung together good day after good day at 49ers camp, and the more reps he gets with Brock Purdy, the harder it is to picture him starting the season anywhere but on the field.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · 49ers Column

Every camp has a rookie who flashes for a day and disappears for a week. That was never going to be the De'Zhaun Stribling story, and by now it's clear why. The chemistry with Brock Purdy that showed up early in camp hasn't faded, it's deepened, and the routes that looked clean for a first-year receiver a couple weeks ago now just look clean, period, no rookie asterisk required. He's not just making the highlight rep of the day anymore. He's the guy consistently winning his rep, day after day, which is the actual signal that separates a camp flash from a real football player.

Here's what makes it so believable: he isn't getting served up easy looks. This receiver room has real veterans in it now. Mike Evans is in the building as a proven X. Christian Kirk is on a shrewd one-year deal. Ricky Pearsall is fighting for his own bigger role. Stribling isn't winning reps because the corner across from him doesn't know the offense yet or because the room is thin and desperate for bodies. He's winning them against real competition for real reps, which is exactly the kind of camp battle a team wants its first-round pick fighting and exactly the kind this one looks like he's winning.

And that's why the "is he a starter" question is starting to feel like the wrong question. The right one is what role he starts in. Purdy trusting him on back-shoulder throws and double moves this early in camp isn't a small thing, that's the kind of rep quarterbacks usually reserve for receivers they've played a full season with. If that trust keeps building the way it has been, Kyle Shanahan doesn't have much of a decision to make. You don't bury production and chemistry like this on the bench because a guy is technically a rookie. You put your best players on the field, and right now Stribling is playing like one of them.

The best part for Niner Nation is that none of this is happening in a vacuum where the offense needs him to be great immediately. Evans, Kirk, Kittle, and a fully healthy McCaffrey mean Stribling gets to keep developing without the offense leaning on him to be the whole answer. But make no mistake, "gets to develop" and "is going to start" are not the same sentence anymore. At the rate he's going, this rookie isn't waiting for an opportunity. He's taking it, one clean rep at a time, and by the time the lights come on for real in September, don't be surprised if De'Zhaun Stribling is just called a starter instead of a rookie who earned it.
