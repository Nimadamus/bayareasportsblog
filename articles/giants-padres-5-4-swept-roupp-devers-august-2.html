Padres 5, Giants 4: Swept in San Diego, Four Walks in the Second, and a Deadline That Cannot Come Fast Enough

Three games in San Diego. Three losses. Seven runs allowed, then six, then five, and a lineup that keeps arriving one run late and one inning short. Forty-seven and sixty-five, and the only thing left worth watching happens Monday in an office.

The second inning was the whole game, again

I have written some version of this paragraph so many times this summer that I could probably do it from memory, and that is the part that has me fed up. The Giants lost 5-4. They did not lose because San Diego is a juggernaut. They lost because Landen Roupp could not throw strikes in the second inning and by the time he got out of it the Padres had four runs and this team does not come back from four runs. Not this year. Not with this lineup.

Ty France singled. Jackson Merrill hit his sixteenth home run to center and it was 2-0 before anybody in the ballpark had gotten comfortable. Then a walk, then another walk, then Gavin Sheets on base, then Jake Cronenworth grounding a two-run single through the left side against a drawn-in infield, and it was 4-0 in the second inning of a game the Giants had to win to avoid a sweep. Roupp finished with 4.2 innings, six hits, five runs, four walks and six strikeouts. Six strikeouts. He had the stuff. He did not have the strike zone, and in 2026 that is a sentence about the whole pitching staff.

They chipped, and chipped, and never once got even

Give the hitters this much: they did not quit. Bryce Eldridge and Rafael Devers both came around in the fourth on a Willy Adames single and a Drew Cavanaugh groundout. Osleivis Basabe — the same Basabe who put up four RBIs on Saturday and has now homered twice in a season he barely got to play in — went deep in the fifth to make it 4-3. Devers hit his twenty-third in the eighth to make it 5-4.

And that is the entire indictment. Three separate times they cut the lead. Zero times they took one. Seven hits, six left on base, two errors in the field, and Luis Arraez going 0-for-5 at the top of the order while Jung Hoo Lee went 2-for-3 behind him. San Diego did not have to do anything hard in the last four innings. They handed the ball to Adrian Morejon, who struck out three in 1.1 innings and never let it get interesting, and the Giants went home.

The bullpen was fine and it did not matter

Sam Hentges, Dylan Smith, JT Brubaker and Erik Miller combined for 3.1 scoreless innings. Not one run. The relievers kept this within one for the entire back half of the ballgame and the offense could not find one more swing. That is the most Giants thing possible — the piece that has burned them all year finally holds up for a night, and the other three pieces fail instead.

And the error column. Two of them. In a one-run loss. Luis Campusano's fifth-inning single that made it 5-3 got through because it deflected off Adames' glove, which is the sort of play that does not go in the book as a run given away but absolutely is one. This team plays like it is tired. It is August 2 and they play like it is October and they have been running on fumes since April, except they were never in a race to be tired from.

Twenty-one and thirty-seven on the road

Twenty-one wins and thirty-seven losses away from Oracle Park. That is a .362 road team. That is not a bad stretch, that is not a slump, that is an identity. Three straight in San Diego by 7-0, 6-5 and 5-4 — a shutout, a ninth-inning giveaway, and a game they were never quite in but never quite out of. Different endings, same ending.

Next up is Texas, which is exactly the kind of series where this team goes and drops two more and nobody notices. Forty-seven and sixty-five. Twenty-two games back in the division. Twelve out of a wild card that stopped being a real conversation weeks ago.

Buster, please. Monday.

The deadline is Monday. I have said it for two weeks and I will say it one more time, because this is the last chance to say it before it either happens or does not. Sell. Move the expiring contracts, take on the prospects, hand the at-bats to Eldridge and Basabe and the kids who are going to be here in 2028, and stop selling us the idea that this group is a tweak away. It is not a tweak away. It got swept by a wild-card team in three straight and scored four runs in the game it actually tried in.

I will be watching Monday. Not because I expect Posey to do the right thing, but because it is the only day left this season where something can actually change. Go Giants, and I mean that through gritted teeth.
