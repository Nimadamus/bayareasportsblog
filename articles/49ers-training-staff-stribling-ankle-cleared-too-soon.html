They Sent Stribling Back Out on a Sprained Ankle. Fire the Training Staff.

Purdy's toe, Pearsall's knee, and now a rookie carted off in his debut after the tent cleared him. I'm done giving the 49ers medical staff the benefit of the doubt.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · 49ers

I've had this one stuck in my chest since Thursday night, and I'm not letting it go.

De'Zhaun Stribling, the kid we took at 33, the one I've been pounding the table for since the draft, hurts his left ankle early in Melbourne. He limps off. He goes into the blue tent. A few minutes later they've looked at it, decided he's fine, and sent him back out there.

He runs a route, plants to make his cut, and goes down grabbing the same ankle.

This time he can't walk off. They bring the cart. In his NFL debut. On the other side of the planet.

Deltoid sprain, out at least a month, and he's out getting more opinions on it. Surgery isn't the plan right now. Nobody's taken it off the table, either, and that's the part that keeps me up.

I watched it on my couch with a plate of wings I never finished. My buddy texted me one word. "Again?" I didn't even have to ask what he meant.

A few minutes in a tent

Dustin Perry runs player health and performance for this team. Timothy McAdams is the team doctor. Those are the people who looked at a rookie's ankle for a few minutes and decided he was good to go.

I'm not a doctor. I know that. I also know the deltoid is the ligament on the inside of your ankle, the one that keeps it from caving in, and the entire job of a wide receiver is planting on that foot and snapping it the other way. What did they think was going to happen on the first hard break? Seriously, I'd love for somebody in that building to walk me through it.

It was Week 1. September. We've got sixteen more of these. There was zero reason to be in a hurry with him. You sit the kid down, you wrap it, you get him in a tube, and you find out what you're dealing with before you let him cut on it. Instead we found out the hard way, on live TV, and he's the one paying for it.

Purdy, Pearsall, and now the rookie

If this were a one off I'd grumble for a day and move on. It isn't.

Last year Brock Purdy hurt his toe in Week 1. Turf toe. Other teams put guys on IR for that and let the thing heal. We had him back out there in Week 4 against Jacksonville, and he looked like a guy playing on one foot, because he basically was. He hurt it again and missed six more weeks. That's our franchise quarterback, the guy we just handed the big contract, getting run back out before he was ready.

Then Ricky Pearsall and that PCL. The timeline they floated was a few weeks. He missed six games, came back clearly not himself, and hurt it again against Tennessee. They went the patient route all offseason, the knee swelled up a few days into camp, and now he's having surgery and his whole 2026 is gone. I wrote in August that the kid just can't catch a break. I'm a lot less sure it was only bad luck.

See the pattern? Guy gets hurt. The team's timeline says he's close. He goes back on that timeline. He gets hurt worse and misses twice as long as if they'd just waited. Stribling's the newest name on the list, and he didn't even get a whole first half.

They had their chance to clean house

After last season I figured somebody in that department was getting walked out of the building. Bosa tore his ACL in Week 3. Warner broke and dislocated his ankle against Tampa. Kittle's year ended with a torn Achilles. It felt like every Monday started with another MRI.

So what'd they do? They spent something like nine million dollars on the facility, a lot of it on hydrotherapy. They hired three more physical therapists. They rearranged camp so nobody practiced three days in a row. And they kept the core training staff right where it was.

Two days into that brand new camp, Christian Kirk had a calf strain and Malik Mustapha had a hamstring. Kirk's on IR right now. Great plan, guys. Love the pools.

I'll be straight with you about my bias, because I've got one. Bosa's knee and Warner's ankle weren't anybody in the tent's fault. Guys get rolled up on. Football breaks people, and I'm not going to blame a trainer for a dislocated ankle at the bottom of a pile.

What I'm blaming them for is the stuff they actually control. Who goes back in. When a guy's ready. How long a sprain really takes. That's the medical staff's whole job, and for years now it keeps missing in the exact same direction, always too early, never too late. When's the last time you heard about a Niner who sat an extra week he didn't need?

I can't think of one.

My timeline's been nothing but this

Go scroll through Niners Twitter since Thursday. It's wall to wall people saying what I'm saying, and a lot of them are way less polite about it. Fire the training staff. Replace the whole medical side. Stop letting these guys decide who's healthy.

And they're right. I've gone back and forth on a lot of things with this team over the years. Not on this.

This is the wrong year to keep guessing

Look at the receiver room right now. Pearsall's done for the year. Kirk's on IR. The rookie's out a month, minimum. Purdy's throwing to Mike Evans, Deebo, and Demarcus Robinson and hoping nothing else pops. Tonges and his knee aren't helping either.

And we've got a real team. Purdy's clean, Bosa's back, and we just flew across the world and beat the Rams by twenty. This is a year you can win the whole thing, and I refuse to watch it get decided in a blue tent by the same people who already showed us what they do with an ankle.

Kyle just got his extension. Whoever's signing off on the medical side in that building, fix it. New people. New process. Somebody who's willing to tell a head coach no. Do something besides buy another cold tub.

And De'Zhaun, if you ever read this, take your time. Get every opinion you want. Don't let anybody in that building tell you you're ready until you know you are.
