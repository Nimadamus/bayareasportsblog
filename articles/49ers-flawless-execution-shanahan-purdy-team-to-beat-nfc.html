The 49ers Are Playing Flawless Football, and They're the Team to Beat in the NFC

One penalty for five yards on Sunday. Zero sacks allowed in four games. One Purdy interception all year. Kyle Shanahan is coaching out of his mind, and I'm done pretending to be cautious about this team.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · 49ers · Published Mon, Oct 5, 2026, 5:15 PM PT

I was folding laundry this morning with the Broncos game on replay in the background (don't judge me, I rewatch every win) and somewhere in the third quarter it hit me that I hadn't heard a flag all day. Not one I could remember. So I went and looked it up, and I had to read it twice.

One penalty. Five yards. The whole game.

Do you know how hard that is? Sixty minutes of NFL football, a defense flying around trying to hit Bo Nix, an offensive line holding up against a front that led the league in sacks last year, and the officials walked off five yards against us all afternoon. I've watched Niners teams that couldn't get out of their own huddle without a false start. I sat through years of that. This doesn't happen, and it happened at Levi's on Sunday while everybody was busy talking about Greenlaw.

Nobody has laid a finger on Brock Purdy

Then there's the sack number, which I keep bringing up to anybody who'll listen. My group chat is sick of me. I don't care.

Zero. Four games, 113 passes, and our quarterback hasn't been sacked one time. Not in Melbourne against the Rams, not against Miami, not in that Arizona shootout, and not against Denver. I genuinely thought the streak would die Sunday. I said so in the preview. I've never been happier to be wrong about anything.

Give the offensive line their flowers, because they've earned every one. But give Brock some too. He's getting the ball out on time, he's sliding away from pressure instead of eating it, and he's throwing it away when there's nothing there. A lot of sacks come from a quarterback holding the ball too long, and he just isn't holding it.

And he isn't giving the ball away either

Here's Brock's line through four games: 79 of 113, 1,007 yards, 11 touchdowns, one interception. That pick came in the opener in Australia, and he hasn't thrown one since. Three straight games and 79 straight attempts without one. His passer rating is sitting at 126. I wrote last week that he's the best quarterback in the NFL and I'd write it again tonight.

The whole team has two giveaways in four games. Two! When you don't get sacked, don't get flagged and don't turn it over, you just don't beat yourself. The other team has to go out and beat you. So far nobody's been good enough to do it.

Kyle Shanahan is coaching out of his mind

Look, I yelled at him at halftime Sunday. Three to three, run, run, short throw, punt, and I put it in writing last night that he was too conservative. I still think the first half was too tight.

But zoom out for a second. This man was in a car wreck in July. A concussion and broken bones, and he was easing back into camp while the rest of the league was installing offense at full speed. Since then he's lost Bosa to a calf, he's had half the receiver room in the trainer's room, and Juszczyk went down Sunday. And he's got us 4 and 0, scoring 30.5 points a game, converting 23 of 40 on third down.

Clean football is coached football. One flag in a game doesn't happen by accident, and neither does a month without a sack. Guys know the snap count. They know the protection. They know where to line up. That comes from meetings and walkthroughs and a head coach who has the whole building locked in on the details, and I don't think we give Kyle enough credit for that part because it isn't flashy.

The flashy part's been fun too, though. Purdy to Mike Evans, a lateral to Deebo, 80 yards against Arizona. I'm still not over that one. Kittle on the screen for 56 to ice Denver. The guy can still draw it up.

So yeah, I'm saying it: team to beat in the NFC

There are two unbeaten teams left in the NFC, us and Minnesota. Kansas City's 4 and 0 over in the AFC. I've watched the Vikings. I'm not scared of the Vikings. They beat a winless Miami team 15 to 10 on Sunday, and we put 35 on that same Miami team two weeks ago.

Through four games we've scored 122 points and given up 64. We went to the other side of the planet and beat the Rams by 20. We survived a shootout with Arizona. We beat a good Denver team with our best pass rusher in street clothes.

I know it's October. I know four games is four games, and I know Seattle's 3 and 1, the defending champs, and waiting for us Sunday at 1:25 in that awful building. I'm not rational about this team and I've never claimed to be. But if they keep playing this clean, no flags, no sacks, no turnovers, I honestly don't know who in this conference beats them.

Somebody tell the offensive line I said thank you. And somebody tell Kyle I take back about half of what I said at halftime. Maybe a third.

Four down. Seattle next. Beat the Seahawks.
