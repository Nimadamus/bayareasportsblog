Cal Preview

Tosh Lupoi Comes Home to Berkeley, and This Cal Team Might Actually Be Dangerous

A first-year head coach who played on this line, a quarterback who threw for 3,454 yards and came back anyway, and a schedule that misses the ACC's three biggest names.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · Cal

Every year somebody tells you Cal is going to be sneaky good and every year you believe it for about six weeks. This one is different in one specific way, and it is the way that usually matters: the quarterback came back.

Jaron-Keawe Sagapolutele threw for 3,454 yards and 18 touchdowns last season and then, in an era where that profile gets a player into a bidding war by Christmas, he stayed. Whatever you think about the state of college football, a franchise quarterback choosing to remain in Berkeley is a data point about the programme that no press release can fake.

The new head coach is Tosh Lupoi, and this is a homecoming. He played defensive line here. He then went and built a serious reputation as an assistant at the college and NFL levels, the kind of coach other coaches hire. Now he gets his own team, at the place he came from, which is the sort of story that either becomes a decade or becomes a cautionary tale and very rarely lands in between.

What has been built around Sagapolutele is the part nobody outside the Bay is talking about: a genuine haul of receivers out of the portal, two new tackles, several backs worth handing it to, and a defence that got rebuilt rather than patched. That is not the roster of a team playing out a rebuild. That is a roster assembled by people who think the window is now.

The schedule agrees. Cal opens 5 September against UCLA, then goes to Syracuse, hosts Wagner and Clemson, travels to UNLV, and takes Virginia Tech and Wake Forest at home before a November road stretch through SMU, NC State and Virginia. The season ends with Stanford on 21 November and Pitt the week after. Look at who is missing: Miami, Louisville and Florida State are all off the card. In a conference this top-heavy, missing the three biggest names is worth more than any recruiting ranking.

The Lupoi hire tells you what kind of team this is meant to be. He is a defensive line coach by trade, and defensive line coaches who become head coaches tend to build the same way: win the line of scrimmage, do not give up cheap points, make the other team's afternoon miserable. Cal has spent years being the sort of opponent that lets a superior team play comfortably. If Lupoi's first team does one thing, it should be to stop being comfortable to play against.

There is a caution worth stating, because this is where every Cal preview goes wrong. A portal haul is not the same as a roster. Bringing in a pile of receivers and two tackles in one offseason means a lot of players learning a new offense at the same time, and those teams tend to look disjointed in September before they look like anything. If this team starts slowly, the temptation locally will be to declare it another false dawn by the end of the month. Do not. The schedule was built so the games that matter come later.

The other thing nobody says out loud: this programme has a history of getting to five wins and stalling, and the difference between five and eight is almost always the quarterback. Cal has a quarterback. That is the entire argument for optimism, and it is a better argument than the one available in most years.

Winnable home games, survivable road trips, a quarterback who wanted to be here, and a coach with something to prove in the town that raised him. If this team does not do something in 2026, the excuses are gone. The full breakdown is in our game-by-game schedule piece, and everything else lives on the Cal hub.
