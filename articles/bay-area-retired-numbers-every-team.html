Bay Area Retired Numbers: Every Team, Every Jersey

Every jersey nobody around here will ever wear again, and who I'd fight you over.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · Bay Area

What a wall. Stand in Oracle Park and look up, or walk Levi's, or sit at Chase Center between quarters, and it's all right there. Every number in this region that nobody's ever going to wear again. I love this stuff. I could stare at retired numbers all day, and I basically did for this page, so here's every one of them, team by team, with who I'd fight you over.

49ers 12 numbers

8Steve Young
12John Brodie
16Joe Montana
34Joe Perry
37Jimmy Johnson
39Hugh McElhenny
42Ronnie Lott
70Charlie Krueger
73Leo Nomellini
79Bob St. Clair
80Jerry Rice
87Dwight Clark

Twelve! And not one of them's a stretch. Clark's 87 went up in 1988, and every time I see it I think about that catch and then about him, and I get a little choked up. Who's next? Patrick Willis wore 52 and I'm ready to march on Santa Clara about it.

Giants 13 numbers, plus two names

3Bill Terry
4Mel Ott
11Carl Hubbell
20Monte Irvin
21Jeff Kent
22Will Clark
24Willie Mays
25Barry Bonds
27Juan Marichal
30Orlando Cepeda
36Gaylord Perry
44Willie McCovey
42Jackie Robinson

That's the newest one up there, Jeff Kent's 21, retired on August 29 this year, and I loved seeing Bonds hug him on the field after all those years. Christy Mathewson and John McGraw are honored too, from the New York days before numbers were a thing. Buster Posey's 28 isn't up yet, and Madison Bumgarner's 40 isn't either. Yet. Please, soon. Jackie's 42 is retired across all of baseball, so you'll see it on the A's wall too.

Warriors 7 numbers

9Andre Iguodala
13Wilt Chamberlain
14Tom Meschery
16Al Attles
17Chris Mullin
24Rick Barry
42Nate Thurmond

Iguodala's the fresh one, up in February of 2025, and that man deserved every second of that night. Here's the crazy part: no Curry yet, because he's still out there hitting threes at 38. When 30 goes up, I'm going to be a mess. The team has also said Klay's 11 will get there, and they said the same about Durant's 35 back in 2019.

Sharks 2 numbers

12Patrick Marleau
19Joe Thornton

Patty went first, in February of 2023, and Jumbo followed in November of 2024. That's it, and that's exactly right for a franchise that's only been around since 1991. Give Macklin Celebrini about 15 years. I'll be there.

A's 6 numbers, plus Haas

9Reggie Jackson
24Rickey Henderson
27Catfish Hunter
34Rollie Fingers
34Dave Stewart
43Dennis Eckersley

Look at that, 34 is on there twice, for Rollie and for Stew, two Oakland legends. They've also got an A up for Walter Haas, the owner who actually loved this town. Every one of these guys earned it in Oakland, and every one of them's now hanging over a team that left. I've got feelings about that.

And the Raiders?

None. Zero.

The Raiders don't retire numbers, officially or unofficially, and never have. It's the most Raiders thing in the world. Al Davis would've told you the number belongs to the team and the next guy had better earn it. I sort of love it, and I'd never admit that to a Raiders fan.

So that's 40 of them across the five teams, not counting Jackie twice, and I'd bet every kid in the Bay Area who ever played Little League wanted one of them. Who's missing? Tell me I'm wrong about Willis. The stat side of the argument is on the 49ers all time leaders page, and you can dig into the legends one by one in the Bay Area Hall of Fame.
