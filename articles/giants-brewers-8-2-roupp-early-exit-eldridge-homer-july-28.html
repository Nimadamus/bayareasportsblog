Brewers 8, Giants 2: Landen Roupp Never Found It, Bryce Eldridge's Homer Was the Only Answer

Landen Roupp couldn't get through four innings, Logan Henderson made sure the Giants couldn't get anything going until it no longer mattered, and Milwaukee just kept hitting. Bryce Eldridge's two-run homer in the eighth was the only thing worth cheering for at Oracle Park. Brewers win 8-2. Giants fall to 45-62.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · Giants Column

This is what it looks like when a fourth-place team in July runs into a first-place lineup that is locked in. Landen Roupp did not have it from the start, and by the time Bob Melvin came to get him he had faced 20 batters, retired 11, and watched Milwaukee build a 4-0 lead that felt like it could have been worse. Jackson Chourio singled home a run in the third, and the fourth inning was the one that buried him: a Cooper Pratt single, a Luis Lara single, and a two-run Brice Turang single turned a manageable deficit into a rout in progress.

Logan Henderson made sure none of it mattered anyway. The Brewers right-hander was nearly untouchable, allowing just two hits over five and two-thirds innings with seven strikeouts, and the Giants lineup that has produced so little for so long could not scratch together a single real threat against him. JT Brubaker came on in relief and gave Milwaukee three more innings of cover while the Brewers offense kept adding on, Lara tripling home two runs in the seventh and Jackson Chourio grounding out to score another in the eighth to push it to 8-0.

By the time Bryce Eldridge stepped in against reliever Grant Anderson in the eighth, the only question left was whether Oracle Park would get anything to remember from the night. Eldridge answered it, driving a pitch 369 feet to right for a two-run homer that scored Luis Arraez and got the Giants on the board, 8-2. It is the kind of swing that has become the one real reason to still watch this team most nights, a 21-year-old doing damage in a game that had been decided for an hour already.

There is not much to take from a night like this beyond what has already been true for weeks: the pitching cannot hold a lineup like Milwaukee's, the offense cannot generate enough against a starter who is dealing, and Eldridge is the one player capable of making a laugher briefly interesting. Roupp falls to 7-9, and the Giants drop to 45-62 with a Brewers lineup that is not letting up anytime soon.
