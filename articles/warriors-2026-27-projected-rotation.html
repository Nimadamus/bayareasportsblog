The Warriors Projected Rotation for 2026-27, and Who Actually Closes

A depth chart says who's on the roster. A rotation says who gets the minutes that matter. Here's the projection, and the four decisions it hinges on.

Bay Area Sports Blog Staff · Warriors

A depth chart tells you who's on the roster. A projected rotation tells you who Steve Kerr actually trusts at nine o'clock on a Tuesday night in January, and those aren't the same list. Here's our projected Warriors rotation for 2026-27, built off the roster as it stands in August, carrying the caveat that hangs over everything with this team now: it assumes a level of health this group hasn't had in two years.

Ten players is roughly where Kerr likes to live before injuries force him deeper. Last season he was forced deeper constantly. Forty three different starting lineups in one year isn't a rotation, it's triage, and the fact that this team still won a play in game before Phoenix ended it at 37-45 says something about how hard the healthy nights were being worked.

The Curry minutes question

Thirty-two minutes a night is the number this staff has protected for a while now, and at thirty-eight it's the right one. The interesting part isn't the total, it's the distribution. Kerr has spent years staggering Curry so that he opens the second and fourth quarters with the bench, because a bench unit with Curry on it's a playoff team and a bench unit without him has historically been an adventure. Expect the same shape: a heavy first quarter, an early rest, a long stretch to open the second, and then whatever the game demands after that.

Who closes

Closing lineups are where a rotation gets honest. Our projection for a tight game in November, with Butler still rehabbing, is Curry, Podziemski, Santos, Green and Porzingis, with Horford swapped in for Porzingis when the Warriors want five out and everything switchable, which has been the Golden State answer to a jam for a decade. Payton closes on nights when the other team has one guard who simply has to be stopped. That's several different fifth men depending on the matchup, and Kerr has never been shy about changing his mind at a timeout.

What changes when Butler is back

Everything. Butler tore his right ACL on 19 January and had surgery on 9 February, and the honest timeline puts him somewhere around February rather than the start of the season. When he returns he doesn't simply slot into Santos's minutes, he takes the ball. Butler on the floor turns Curry from the only creator into a player who can move without the ball for a full possession, which is the version of this offense the front office thought it was buying. Santos drops to sixteen minutes, Richard probably falls out on healthy nights, and the rotation tightens to nine.

Moody is the other returning piece. Torn left patellar tendon on 23 March, nine to twelve months, with on court work targeted for September. If he's right by the trade deadline he's a starter's worth of wing defence and catch and shoot volume arriving off the bench, and he's the difference between a rotation that has enough wings and one that doesn't.

The rookie minutes

Yaxel Lendeborg went eleventh out of Michigan and there's a version of this season where he's playing twenty minutes a night by February. There's also a version where he plays nine. This coaching staff has a long history of preferring the known quantity in a tight game, which is the entire Kuminga argument compressed into one sentence, and a team paying this much money to win right now isn't a natural environment for rookie development. Sixteen minutes is the projection and neither side of it would surprise us.

The frontcourt problem nobody wants to say out loud

Porzingis and Horford are the two best bigs on this roster and neither is a safe bet for seventy five games. Porzingis carries a real medical history, including the POTS diagnosis that wiped out most of a season in Boston, and Horford is forty years old. Rest at centre isn't a maybe here, it's a scheduling assumption, and third centre minutes matter more on this roster than they do on almost any other team in the league. That's the quiet reason Green will spend more time at the five than anybody would like.

What we are actually watching in camp

Four things. Whether Santos holds the starting job or Lendeborg takes it off him. Whether Podziemski is a thirty minute player on a good team or a nice piece on a bad one. How many minutes the staff is willing to play Green at centre. And whether Melton, who has his own injury history, can hold twenty minutes a night as the backup guard, because if he can't then the entire second unit has to be redrawn before Christmas.

The roster itself, position by position, is on the depth chart page. The money that produced this rotation is on the cap sheet page. The season opens on 21 October at the Lakers and the home opener is 23 October against Memphis, and the argument about what this team really is lives in the season outlook.
