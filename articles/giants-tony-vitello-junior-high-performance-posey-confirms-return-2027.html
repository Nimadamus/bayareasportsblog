Tony Vitello Called It a Junior High Performance, and Buster Posey Just Confirmed He Is Back Next Year

He isn't wrong about what he watched. That's the problem. The man describing junior high baseball is the man who filled out the lineup card, ran the pitching staff, and is now guaranteed to be doing it again in April.

The quote is the most accurate thing anybody in that clubhouse has said all year

Junior high. That's the word he used about his own baseball team after they handed a game to the worst club in the National League, and I have to give him this much, it's the first time all season that a description out of that room actually matched what my eyes were reporting.

Because that's what it looked like. Seven to six going to the sixth, and then seven unanswered. Rafael Devers hit his twenty fifth and it went straight into the memory hole about forty minutes later. Thirty two thousand people paid real money on a Sunday to sit in that ballpark and watch a professional roster come apart in front of Colorado. Junior high is generous. Junior high teams usually know where to throw the ball.

So no, I'm not mad at him for saying it out loud. I'm tired of managers standing at a podium doing the tough loss, we battled, they're a better team than their record routine. He skipped all of that and told the truth. Good. That's the bar now, apparently, and he cleared it.

Here is the part nobody wants to sit with

He's describing his own team. Not a team he inherited last week. Not a team somebody handed him at the deadline. His team, in the middle of August, in year one, playing the kind of baseball that makes a manager reach for a middle school comparison in public.

When a manager says the effort and the execution were junior high, he's saying one of two things. Either these players quit on him, or nobody ever taught them how to do this correctly. Both of those answers land on his desk. That's the entire job. You aren't the guy who hits the baseball, you're the guy responsible for what the team looks like when it's losing, and this team has looked exactly like this for months.

Fifty one and seventy three. Twenty two and a half games out. Fourth place in the West. Those aren't vibes, those are the standings, and no quote fixes them.

We have been writing about this since spring

None of this is a surprise around here. We wrote in the spring that he wasn't ready and he basically told us so himself, back when the whole story was about a college coach learning that big leaguers don't respond to being talked down to. We wrote in the summer about lineups that made no sense at all, hitters slotted in places that helped nobody, a rookie leading off because somebody upstairs likes the idea of it.

You can survive a rough first year as a manager. Plenty of good ones have had one. What you can't survive with this fan base is a year where the roster underperforms, the in game decisions look improvised, and the only memorable soundbite of the season is the manager calling his own players children. That isn't a rebuilding year. That's a room that lost its temperature.

And then Posey confirmed it

Buster Posey confirmed Vitello is coming back next season. There it's. No search, no interviews, no October where we all pretend to be interested in a list of names. He's the manager in 2027 and the front office wanted everybody to know it now.

I understand the logic. I do. You don't hire a guy out of college baseball, hand him a roster in transition, and then fire him in August of his first year because the bullpen can't hold a one run lead. That's how organizations get a reputation for being a meat grinder, and nobody good takes the job after that. Posey is playing a long game and part of a long game isn't blinking in public.

Here's my problem with the timing. You don't announce it in the middle of a stretch like this. Not the same week the manager goes on television and calls his own team junior high. All that does is tell every player in that clubhouse that the guy running the room can say whatever he wants about them and nothing changes, and it tells the fan base that the plan for next April is the same guy running the same operation with the same coaching staff.

If he's the guy, then say it in October with a plan attached. Tell us what changes. Tell us who's coaching the hitters, tell us what happens with the bullpen, tell us why year two is different. A vote of confidence with nothing behind it's just a press release.

What I actually want

I want to be wrong about him. I genuinely do. Everything I read about the guy says he can coach, that players who play hard for him love him, that he built something real in college and he didn't do that by accident. There's a version of this where the fire is exactly what this organization needed after years of polite mediocrity, and in two seasons we're all laughing about the junior high quote as the moment it turned.

But this is the season we've actually been watching, and it has been miserable. Logan Webb keeps taking the ball like it matters because that's who he's. Devers keeps hitting. Everything else has been a slow leak since May.

So Tony Vitello gets his second year, whether we like it or not. He earned the right to say what he said, because somebody in that building needed to say it. Now he has about six weeks left to prove he can also fix it, because the quote doesn't mean anything if the same team plays the same way next April.

Junior high, he said. He's right. He's also the principal.
