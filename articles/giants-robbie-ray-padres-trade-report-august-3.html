Robbie Ray to the Padres: Of Course It Is the Padres. Of Course It Is.

Nothing is official as I write this. No announcement, no physical, no press conference, none of it. But the word is out that Robbie Ray is going to San Diego, and I want you to sit with which team that is for a second, because I have been sitting with it all morning and it has not gotten better.

The Padres. Not a mystery team. The Padres.

We spent three days in Petco Park getting our teeth kicked in. Shut out, then a one-run giveaway, then swept on Sunday by a club that never had to leave second gear. I wrote about all three because I watched all three. And now, two days later, the report going around is that we are handing them our best healthy left-handed starter for the stretch run.

I am not going to pretend the baseball logic is insane. It is not. Ray was on an expiring deal, this team is eighteen games under .500, and I have been screaming sell in this space for weeks. I asked for this. I did. I just did not think it would go to the one address on the map that is going to make me feel it twice a week for the rest of my life.

Let me say the part I am not supposed to say

A former Cy Young winner. On our team. In our rotation. Behind Webb he was the only guy I felt calm about, the only start where I did not have my jaw clenched by the third inning, and now he is going to be the guy grinning in a brown jersey in October while we watch it on a television in a house in the Bay Area with nothing on the line.

And it is going to look good on him. That is the part that gets me. He is going to a place with a real lineup behind him and a real bullpen behind that, and he is going to look like exactly the pitcher we kept telling ourselves we had. Same arm. Same guy. Different uniform, and suddenly it counts.

This is what selling actually feels like, and I hate it

Here is my confession. I asked for the teardown. I said it in print two days ago. Sell the expiring guys, get the at-bats to the kids, stop pretending. Every word of that is still right and I would write it again this morning.

But there is a difference between wanting a thing in the abstract and watching it happen, and the watching part is worse. Because selling is not a strategy when you do it every other summer. It is a habit. We are the team that gets good enough to be interesting, misses, sells, and starts over, and the teams we sell to are the teams that go win with our guys.

Now the return had better be real

I do not know what is coming back. Nobody has said, and I am not going to make something up to fill a paragraph. What I know is what the standard is: if this is a lottery ticket and a handshake, if it is an org arm and a bat nobody has ever ranked, then Buster did the hard part and flinched on the easy part.

Trading a Cy Young winner on an expiring deal to a division rival in a pennant race is a leverage position. They need pitching. They need it now. That is the one time all year the Giants have actually had something somebody wanted, and I want to see it turned into somebody who matters in 2028, not salary relief and a name I will forget by Thursday.

The rest of today

If Ray is going, then Arraez should be going, and the veteran relievers on expiring deals should be going, and Ramos goes only if somebody pays properly for him. Half a teardown is the worst outcome available. Half a teardown is how you end up 79-83 next year telling everybody you are close.

Webb stays. Eldridge plays. Basabe plays. That part I actually feel fine about, which is the strangest sentence I have written all summer.

Until it is announced, it is not announced

Deals fall apart on deadline day. Physicals happen. Names change on the way to the finish line. So I am telling you what everybody is hearing and I am not telling you it is done, because it is not done yet.

But if it is real, then the first time Robbie Ray takes the mound against us in orange-and-black weather at Oracle in a brown uniform, I am going to be sitting there frustrated and quiet and fully aware I asked for this. Go Giants. Somebody get a return worth the aggravation.
