The Story

The Klay Thompson Story

Every Warriors fan I know has a Klay night. Mine's a Friday in January 2015, watching a guy who barely changed expressions turn Oracle into a madhouse, and the thing I remember most is how calm he looked while the rest of us lost our minds. That was always the deal with Klay. We'd be screaming. He'd be jogging back on defense like he'd just hit a layup in a pickup game.

He was born in Los Angeles on February 8, 1990, and he grew up around the league. His dad, Mychal Thompson, was the first overall pick in the 1978 draft. His brother Mychel played a little in the NBA and his brother Trayce made the big leagues in baseball. The family moved to Ladera Ranch when Klay was 14, and he graduated from Santa Margarita Catholic in 2008.

Washington State got him for three seasons. He was a two time first team all conference pick, and as a junior he led the Pac 10 in scoring. Then the Warriors took him 11th overall in the 2011 draft, which in hindsight is one of the best things this franchise has ever done on a draft night. Nobody threw a parade at the time. We should have.

He started his rookie year on the bench. Then the Warriors traded Monta Ellis in March 2012, the job was his, and he finished the year on the All Rookie First Team. By 2012/13 he and Steph Curry were combining for more threes than any duo the league had ever seen, and somebody started calling them the Splash Brothers. It stuck because it was true. In the second round against San Antonio that spring he went for 34 in a playoff game and hit 8 of 9 from deep. I remember thinking, okay, this kid isn't a role player.

Then the run started. On January 23, 2015 he scored 37 points in the third quarter against Sacramento, 13 for 13 from the field, nine threes, an NBA record for a quarter that still makes me laugh out loud when I think about it. He made his first All Star team that winter, and in June the Warriors beat Cleveland in six for the franchise's first title in 40 years. Klay was 25 and already part of the best backcourt in the sport.

The 2015/16 team won 73 games, and Klay won the Three Point Contest at All Star weekend, beating Steph and Devin Booker in the final round. Then he saved the season. Down 3 to 1 to Oklahoma City in the West finals, he went to their building for Game 6 and hit 11 threes, a playoff record at the time, and scored 41. We won Game 7. He made 98 threes that postseason, tied for the most ever. And then we blew the 3 to 1 lead in the Finals, and I'm not going to talk about that.

Kevin Durant came, and Klay just kept doing Klay things. Sixty points against Indiana on December 5, 2016 in 29 minutes, which nobody had ever done in under 30 minutes. Titles in 2017 and 2018. Thirty five points and nine threes in Game 6 against Houston in 2018 when we were one loss from going home. Fourteen threes in Chicago in October 2018, an NBA record. And through all of it he guarded the other team's best guard, which is how he finally made the All Defensive Second Team in 2019.

Then Game 6 of the 2019 Finals. The last game ever at Oracle. He had 30 points when he went down on a fast break late in the third quarter with a torn ACL, and he walked back out to shoot his two free throws before he left. We lost by four. That summer he signed a five year deal to stay, and then he missed the whole next season with the knee and the whole season after that with a torn Achilles from a pickup game in Los Angeles. Two straight years of nothing. Some of us honestly wondered if we'd ever see him play again.

We did. January 9, 2022, 941 days after the knee, Chase Center lost its mind when he checked in against Cleveland. He scored 17. By May he was Game 6 Klay again, dropping 30 with eight threes to close out Memphis, and in June the Warriors won the title in Boston in six games. Four rings. I cried a little at that one and I'm fine admitting it.

The last two Warriors seasons were harder. He led the league with 301 threes in 2022/23 and led the league in free throw percentage in 2023/24, but he was coming off the bench by February 2024, and his final game in a Warriors uniform was a play in loss in Sacramento in April where he went 0 for 10. That hurt to watch. In July 2024 he left for Dallas in a six team sign and trade. Steph said it sucked. It did.

He played two seasons for the Mavericks. Nights in Dallas included passing Reggie Miller and then Damian Lillard on the all time threes list, which puts him fourth ever as of 2026, and the night he came back to Chase Center as a visitor in November 2024, got a tribute video, and hit six threes on us anyway. In August 2026 he agreed to a buyout with Dallas and signed with the Miami Heat, chasing a fifth ring at 36.

The Warriors have already said they'll retire his number 11 at Chase Center when the time comes, and Steve Kerr has said he'll have a statue outside the building someday. Whatever uniform he's wearing this winter, around here he's ours. Thirteen years, 2,481 threes as a Warrior, second only to Steph in franchise history. Every one of them went in like he expected it to.

Under Pressure

Why He Was Clutch

Klay's whole reputation in the big moments comes down to one phrase fans still say out loud: Game 6 Klay. It wasn't a marketing line. It's what happened when the season was on the line and everybody else on the floor got tight.

Look at the elimination games. Oklahoma City in 2016, down 3 to 1, on the road, down eight going into the fourth quarter, and he scored 19 in that fourth including the go ahead three with a minute and a half left. Houston in 2018, down 3 to 2, down 17 early, and he hit nine threes in a 29 point blowout. Memphis in 2022 wasn't an elimination game for us, but it was a closeout, and he put up 30 with eight threes to end it.

And he did all of it while guarding the other team's best perimeter player most nights. That's the part casual fans miss. Steph got the spotlight. Klay got Lillard, Irving, Harden, whoever, and then still had enough in his legs to fire away from 28 feet in the fourth quarter.

He wasn't perfect, and I won't pretend he was. There were cold playoff series, and his last Warriors game was 0 for 10 in a play in loss. But when people ask me who I'd want shooting with the season on the line, a guy who hit his free throws on a torn ACL in the NBA Finals is a pretty easy answer.

“I was one of the luckiest NBA players ever to play with Michael Jordan, Tim Duncan, David Robinson and some of the greatest players ever. As many spectacular things as Michael did, which he did nightly, I never saw him do that.”

Steve Kerr, Warriors head coach
after Thompson's 37 point quarter against Sacramento, January 23, 2015

The Big Moments

Clutch Moments

  1. January 23, 2015

    Thirty seven in a quarter

    At Oracle against Sacramento, Thompson scored 37 points in the third quarter, going 13 for 13 from the field with nine threes, both NBA records for a quarter. The Warriors outscored the Kings 41 to 22 in that period and won 126 to 101. He finished with 52 points, 11 threes, in under 33 minutes.

  2. May 28, 2016

    Game 6 Klay is born in Oklahoma City

    Down 3 games to 1 in the Western Conference Finals and trailing 83 to 75 after three quarters in Oklahoma City, Thompson scored 19 points in the fourth. His three with 1:35 left broke a 101 to 101 tie, and he finished with 41 points and 11 threes, a playoff record at the time. The Warriors won 108 to 101 and took Game 7 two nights later.

  3. December 5, 2016

    Sixty in 29 minutes

    Against Indiana at Oracle, Thompson scored 60 points in 29 minutes over three quarters, shooting 21 for 33 with eight threes and 10 for 11 at the line. He had 40 at halftime. No player had ever scored 60 in under 30 minutes, and it was the first 60 point game by a Warrior since Rick Barry in 1974. Golden State won 142 to 106.

  4. May 26, 2018

    Saving the season against Houston

    Facing elimination in Game 6 of the Western Conference Finals at Oracle, the Warriors fell behind by 17 early and trailed 39 to 22 after one quarter. Thompson hit nine threes and scored 35 as Golden State held Houston to nine points in the fourth and won 115 to 86. The Warriors won Game 7 in Houston and went on to their third title in four years.

  5. October 29, 2018

    Fourteen threes in Chicago

    At the United Center, Thompson made 14 of 24 three point attempts, breaking the NBA single game record of 13 held by Steph Curry. He had 10 threes in the first half alone and finished with 52 points in about 27 minutes in a 149 to 124 win over the Bulls.

  6. June 13, 2019

    Free throws on a torn ACL

    In Game 6 of the NBA Finals, the final game ever played at Oracle, Thompson was fouled by Danny Green on a fast break with 2:22 left in the third quarter and tore his left ACL coming down. He limped back out, made both free throws to put the Warriors up 85 to 80, and then left for good with 30 points on 8 for 12 shooting. Toronto won 114 to 110 to take the title.

  7. January 9, 2022

    941 days later

    Thompson played his first game since the 2019 Finals against Cleveland at Chase Center, after missing two full seasons with the ACL and then a torn Achilles. He scored 17 points in 20 minutes, hitting three threes, and the Warriors won 96 to 82.

  8. May 13, 2022

    Closing out Memphis

    In Game 6 of the Western Conference Semifinals at Chase Center, with the Warriors clinging to a 78 to 77 lead after three quarters, Thompson finished with 30 points, eight rebounds and eight threes as Golden State won the fourth quarter 32 to 19 and took the series 4 to 2 with a 110 to 96 win. They won the championship a month later.
