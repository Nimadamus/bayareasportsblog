The 49ers, going into 2026

This is the 49ers hub: training camp, injuries, roster moves, and the columns that come out of them. August 2026 has a simple shape. The quarterback is playing the best football of his career and the medical staff is busier than the coaching staff.

Brock Purdy is 147 attempts from the highest career passer rating in NFL history, and camp has looked like it: seventy-yard touchdowns to receivers nobody has heard of, a dime to Demarcus Robinson in the back of the end zone, and real chemistry with rookie De'Zhaun Stribling.

The other half is the injury list. Ricky Pearsall is out for the year before a single kickoff, the receiver room keeps thinning, and Kyle Shanahan is coaching while he recovers from a car accident that was worse than it first sounded. The front office response was four signings in a week, plus Deebo Samuel coming home on a one-year deal.

For the argument about how good this can be, there is the case that this is the best roster Shanahan has ever had, and the reason to be careful about it. The dynasty years, the Montana-Young era and the Super Bowl that still stings are in Bay Area Dynasties and History.

The 2026 season

Four pages carry the season and get updated as it goes: the preview, which lays out the roster and the four questions that decide the year; the schedule hub, where every weekly preview and reaction gets linked as it publishes; the roster and depth chart, position by position with the injuries; and the Purdy page, which tracks where he actually ranks and what he can still reach.

The season opens somewhere absurd: a Week 1 divisional game against the Rams at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, the first regular season game the NFL has ever played in Australia. For the history rather than the present, start with the dynasty years and the Montana-Young split.
