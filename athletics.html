The A's after 2026, still in West Sacramento

This is the Athletics hub. Fifty-seven years of Oakland baseball are boxed up and parked in a Triple-A ballpark while everyone waits on Las Vegas, and that is the whole ugly story. The baseball happening inside it deserves writing about anyway, so we do.

The 2026 season was a mirage that ended on schedule, at 64 and 98 and fourth in the AL West. They were over .500 in June, then came the streaks: nine straight in July, 41-55 at the break, and a free fall that never actually stopped. The one night that broke the pattern, a 15-1 win that snapped a ten-game skid, was worth checking the box score three times.

There is real talent here, which is what makes it maddening. Jacob Lopez keeps pitching well enough to win and keeps getting nothing behind him. Nick Kurtz and Shea Langeliers both started an All-Star Game. Gage Jump, Jacob Wilson, Brian Serven and Tyler Soderstrom all show up in these recaps for the right reasons.

Every game gets covered here, wins and losses. The A's dynasty years and the Moneyball era are in Bay Area Dynasties; the rest of the league is in Bay Area MLB.

The situation, explained

Four pages cover what is actually happening to this franchise, and they get updated as it moves: what Sutter Health Park is really like, 14,014 seats, the wind, and the second-highest home run rate in the majors; the Oakland to Sacramento to Las Vegas timeline, including where the $2 billion Strip ballpark actually stands; the roster and depth chart, read by who is still here in 2028; and what the Bay Area actually lost, written down before somebody rewrites it.

The anger lives in Bay Area Villains, where it belongs. The 2026 season itself is in the first-half breakdown and the free fall that never stopped.
